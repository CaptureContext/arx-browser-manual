Click the button at the end of the address bar, next to the link button, to open the site menu for the page you're on. Both buttons show whenever a tab is selected, and go away when none is. In a split, each pane's header has its own pair, which act on that pane. VoiceOver also lists **Copy URL** and **Site information** in the address bar's actions.

## Buttons

Two rows of four buttons sit at the top. Rest the pointer on one to see its name. A button that can't act on the page, such as Reading Mode on a page without an article, is dimmed. The first row acts on the page once:

- **Share** opens the macOS share menu with the page's address as it is, as **File → Share…** and the page's right-click menu do. To share a cleaned-up or proxy link instead, use [the link commands](/profiles/links).
- **Capture Area** (the camera) lets you pick a block or drag an area on the page first. See [Capture a page](/pages/capture).
- **Capture Page** (the frame with lines) captures what's on screen, as **File → Capture Visible Area…** does.
- **Hide Element…** starts [Hide Element](/pages/content-blocking#hide-an-element-on-a-site) on the page. It's dimmed where blocking is off for the site.

The second row changes how the page shows:

- **Picture in Picture** floats the page. See [Picture in Picture](/pages/media).
- **Reading Mode**, with its feature flag on, shows the page's article over the page. See [Reading mode](/pages/reading-mode).
- **Open in WebKit** and **Dark page** switch something on or off for the site. When one is on, its icon and the inside of the button turn your accent color, and its name on hover says **On**. Clicking one keeps the menu open, so you see it change. See [Open in WebKit](#open-in-webkit) below. **Dark page** works while dark pages are on in **Settings → Appearance & Sound**, and is dimmed otherwise: on, the site's pages are darkened; off, the site keeps its own look.

These buttons are commands too: they're in the menu bar, the command bar and [Settings → Shortcuts](/start/keyboard-shortcuts), where you can give them keys.

## Extensions

The row lists the profile's enabled extensions with their icons, except parts of the browser with nothing to open, such as Chromium's PDF viewer. An extension without one shows its first letter. Rest the pointer on an extension to see its name, and on the row to show **Manage**, which opens the profile's extensions in Settings. Clicking an extension runs it for the page, as clicking its toolbar button does in Chrome and Arc: its popup opens in a small window under it (see [An extension's popup](/profiles/extensions#an-extensions-popup)), or, for an extension without a popup, it gets the click and does its thing on the page. **+** opens the Chrome Web Store in a new [Little Arx](/tabs/little-arx) of the page's profile, in front.

When an extension can't run on the page (it needs the page reloaded to get access, it opens a side panel, which Arx doesn't have, or it's uBlock Origin, which is built into Arx), clicking it opens the profile's extensions on that extension instead.

Right-click an extension for its menu:

| Item | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Open Options | Opens the extension's options page, when it has one |
| Turn Off | Turns the extension off in this profile. Settings › Extensions shows it as disabled straight away; turn it back on there. |
| Allow in Private Windows | Lets the extension run in [private windows](/tabs/private-windows) opened from this profile. Off until you turn it on. |
| Manage Extensions | Opens the profile's extensions in Settings, on this one |
| Remove… | Asks first, under the extensions, then removes it from this profile with its data |

Extensions built into Arx, such as uBlock Origin for [content blocking](/pages/content-blocking), have no Turn Off, Allow in Private Windows or Remove…. uBlock Origin always runs in private windows.

On an extension's page in the store, **Add to Arx** installs it into this profile after Arx's review. See [Chrome Web Store](/profiles/extensions#chrome-web-store).

## Site settings

**Block ads and trackers** turns [content blocking](/pages/content-blocking) off for this site in this profile, or back on. It shows while content blocking is on in Settings.

**Hidden Elements** lists what you've hidden on the site with [Hide Element](/pages/content-blocking#hide-an-element-on-a-site), each with **Show**, which brings it back and reloads the page. The **Hide Element…** button at the top starts Hide Element on the page, whose toast lists the same entries.

Then one row per permission: camera, microphone, location, notifications, pop-ups and redirects, and the clipboard. Each shows what the site gets now, such as **Ask**. Click it to see the choices: **Default** comes first and says in brackets what the profile's default gives, such as **Default (Ask)**, then **Allow**, **Block** and **Ask**. The check is on what the site has: **Default** while it follows the profile, or its own setting. Pick **Allow**, **Block** or **Ask** to give the site its own setting, or **Default** to remove it. Pop-ups and redirects take no Ask. A setting your organization or an extension decides can't be changed.

**Unread badges** works the same way for the site's [unread badges](/tabs/sidebar): it shows **Show** or **Hide**, and its choices are **Default (Show)** or **Default (Hide)**, **Show** and **Hide**.

If the site has its own page zoom, a **Zoom** row shows it, and **Reset** goes back to the profile's default.

## Devices

If the site may use a USB, HID, serial or Bluetooth device you connected earlier, a **Devices** row lists each one. **Forget** takes it back: the site has to ask again. See [USB, Bluetooth and serial devices](/pages/devices).

## Open in WebKit

For https sites, the **Open in WebKit** button adds the site to **Settings → Advanced → WebKit** and moves the page to a WebKit page, where Netflix, Spotify and other protected video and music play. Click it again to take the site off the list and move the page back to Chromium. While a page is in WebKit, the menu lists under the buttons what WebKit pages don't have. The button is on whenever the page is in WebKit, even when its site isn't on the list. On Netflix, Disney+ and the other built-in streaming services, a Chromium page that stayed there after **Back** says Arx offers to open it in WebKit when a video starts. Turned off on one of them, the button stays off: the site opens in Chromium until you turn it back on. On a page that isn't https, the button is dimmed. See [Netflix, Spotify and protected video](/pages/protected-video).

These are the same settings as in [Site settings](/profiles/site-settings), for this site and this profile only.

## Connection

The bottom row says **Secure** for an https page and **Not Secure** for an http one, then the site's name. Pages that aren't on the web, such as a PDF file, say **Local Page**.

The **…** menu has:

| Item | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Clear Cache | Removes this site's cached images and files |
| Clear Cookies | Removes this site's cookies and the other data it stores, which signs you out of it |
| Manage Extensions | Opens the profile's extensions in Settings |
| Add Extension | Opens the Chrome Web Store in a new Little Arx of the page's profile |
| All Site Settings | Opens the profile's [Site settings](/profiles/site-settings) |

Clear Cache and Clear Cookies ask first and name the site they'll clear. That's the site's main domain with all its subdomains: on `mail.example.co.uk` it's `example.co.uk`. They clear all time, in this profile only. Other sites and profiles keep their data. To clear a whole profile, use [Clear browsing data](/profiles/clear-browsing-data).

## Private windows

In a private window the menu has its buttons (Hide Element and Dark page are dimmed there), the connection, and the [extensions](/profiles/extensions#private-windows) you allow in private windows. Click one to use it on the page, as in a regular window. Their tiles have no menu there, and there's no Add Extension or Manage. Extensions that can't run in private windows are named under the others. The menu shows no site settings, though the window follows some of its profile's site settings. See [Private windows](/tabs/private-windows). Its cookies go when you close it. [Content blocking](/pages/content-blocking) still works there.

## Extensions page

Type `arx://extensions` in the address bar or the command bar, or choose **Window → Extensions**, to open the current profile's extensions in Settings.
