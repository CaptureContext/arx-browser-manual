## Media

Media lives in the sidebar: open the **Library** and choose **Media**, or use **Window → Media**. It lists what's playing or paused in the current space's profile, the site's icon and title on each row, in the order the media was opened. Muting, pausing or playing never moves a row. The player at the bottom of the sidebar opens into the same list when you hover it; see [What's playing](/tabs/sidebar#whats-playing). For each one you can:

- Click the row to go back to its tab.
- Play or pause, and mute or unmute, with the buttons at the end of the row.
- Drag the slider under the row's title to turn that tab down, without changing the site's own volume.
- Right-click the row to **Present in Picture in Picture**, **Return Video to Page**, or turn on **Open PiP automatically when this video becomes hidden** for that site.

A call, such as Google Meet, shows "In a call" and adds its own buttons to the row: mute or unmute the microphone, turn the camera on or off, and **Leave Call**. They show the state the site reports.

Right-click a video on the page for **Loop**, **Show Controls** and **Copy Video Frame**, and **Picture in Picture**, which opens it in PiP (or brings it back) while it's listed in Media. Audio has Loop and Show Controls.

A muted tab keeps its row, so you can unmute it there. You can also mute a tab or change its volume from its speaker or its menu in the sidebar; see [A tab's sound](/tabs/sidebar#a-tabs-sound). When nothing is playing, Media says so. Each profile only sees its own media, and private windows aren't listed.

## Full screen

When a page asks for full screen, such as YouTube's full-screen button or its `f` key, Arx shows only that page across the whole screen: no sidebar, toolbar or other split pane. A short "Press Esc to exit full screen" note appears at the top.

- Press **Esc**, or use the page's own button, to leave. The window comes back exactly as it was.
- If the window wasn't in macOS full screen, it enters it for the page and leaves it again afterwards, back at its size and on its desktop. If you'd already put it in full screen, it stays there.
- A playing video stays in full screen while the window moves to its full-screen desktop. It doesn't drop back into the page or float into PiP.
- Press **⌘S** to show the sidebar beside the page. The page keeps filling the rest of the screen. Press **⌘S** again to hide it. Your sidebar setting for the window doesn't change.
- Switching to another tab or space, closing the tab or window, or taking the window out of macOS full screen also leaves.
- A page in Peek fills the window it was opened from. A page in Little Arx gets a full-screen space of its own, like a browser window, which brings Arx to the front.

Private windows work the same way.

## PiP

PiP (Picture in Picture) is Arx's floating video window. It shows one video at a time, in a corner of the screen with rounded corners, 16 points from the screen's edges, and opens in the bottom-right corner.

A playing video floats into PiP on its own when:

- you leave its tab: you pick another tab or space, or another tab takes its pane in a split view. In a split view, only the video whose pane goes away floats;
- its window leaves the screen: you minimise the window or hide Arx;
- its window is in full screen, on its own or side by side with another app in Split View, and you switch to another desktop or to another app in full screen.

A window that isn't in full screen never floats its video when you switch desktops: the video keeps playing where it is, ready when you come back. Switching to another app while the video's window stays on screen doesn't float it either, even when the other app's window covers it.

Come back to Arx with the video's window in front, or go back to its tab, and it returns to the page. A video that was already paused, a site you turned off, and private windows never float on their own. A video that floats while you're in another app never replaces one that's already in PiP.

You can also present a video in PiP yourself:

- Right-click a tab that's playing a video and choose **Present Media in Picture in Picture**.
- Right-click the player at the bottom of the sidebar, or a row in **Media**, and choose **Present in Picture in Picture**. The same menu has **Play** or **Pause**, **Mute** or **Unmute**, and **Return to Tab**.
- Choose **Window → Present Media in Picture in Picture**, or find it in the command bar, for the tab you're looking at.

"Present" means the tab stays where it is: Picture in Picture only shows it. When a video can't go into PiP, such as a protected movie or a site that turns Picture in Picture off, the item is greyed out and its tooltip says why. **Present Tab in Picture in Picture** still floats the whole page.

PiP stays in front on every desktop, and on top of apps in full screen, Arx's own windows or another app's, like the Mac's own Picture in Picture. With more than one display, it stays on the display you put it on. Clicking it doesn't bring Arx's windows forward, and it stays when you hide Arx.

- Drag the video, or drag with two fingers on the trackpad, to move it. Let go and it settles in the nearest corner. Flick it and it carries on in that direction, so a quick flick is enough to send it to the other side, or to a corner of the display next to it. However hard you flick it, it stays on the screen.
- Grab it again while it's still moving to stop it there.
- When you connect or disconnect a display, or change the displays' arrangement or resolution, it goes back to its corner, or to the nearest corner of the display it's on.
- Pinch to resize it. It keeps the video's shape and stays on the screen. You can also drag its edges.
- With **Reduce motion** turned on in the Accessibility settings of macOS, it glides into its corner without bouncing.
- Video calls that use the browser's own Picture in Picture, such as Google Meet, open there too.

### Controls

Move the pointer over the video to show its controls. The video darkens a little, more towards its top and bottom edges, so the white buttons stay readable. The button under the pointer gets a soft circle behind it.

- **Close** is at the top left. It closes PiP and pauses the video.
- At the top right: captions, mute (a crossed-out speaker while the video is muted) and **Back to Tab**, which puts the video back in its page.
- In the middle: play or pause, between going back and forward 10 seconds.
- Along the bottom: the time played on the left, the video's length on the right, and the progress bar under them. Drag the bar to jump to another point. A live stream shows a red **LIVE** badge instead of the times.

### Calls

When you leave a call's tab, or its window leaves the screen, the whole page floats in [Page in Picture in Picture](#page-in-picture-in-picture), so you keep the site's own controls and can still click them: Google Meet keeps its microphone, camera and hang-up buttons. Go back to the call's tab and the page goes back into it, as a video does. In Little Arx, the Little Arx window itself floats. Switching to another app with the call's window still on screen leaves it where it is. A page already floating stays: Arx doesn't replace it. The same automatic Picture in Picture setting, and the same switch for each site, turn this off.

### One tab's own choice

Right-click a tab and open **Picture in Picture** to set how that one tab behaves, whatever its site's setting:

- **Open Automatically**: **As the Site Is Set** (the default), **Always** or **Never**.
- **Show**: **Video, or the Page for a Call** (the default), **The Video** or **The Whole Page** in [Page in Picture in Picture](#page-in-picture-in-picture).

The choice stays with the tab until you close it. Private windows don't offer it, because their media never floats on its own.

In **Settings → General**:

| Setting | Default |
| --- | --- |
| Open Picture in Picture when leaving a tab with video | On |
| Close Picture in Picture when returning to its tab | On |

## Page in Picture in Picture

When a site's own Picture in Picture doesn't work, such as a protected movie, a site that turns it off, or a chat or dashboard you want to keep an eye on, float the whole page instead. Press :keys{combo="⌥ ⌘ P"}, choose **Window → Present Tab in Picture in Picture** or **Picture in Picture** in the site menu, or find it in the command bar. Do it again to bring the page back.

You can also right-click any tab in the sidebar, a Favorite or a tab in a Split View and choose **Present Tab in Picture in Picture**. The tab doesn't need to be the one you're looking at: it stays where it is, and Picture in Picture only shows it.

It starts when you ask, or on its own for a call (see [Calls](#calls)), and going back to the tab doesn't end it. The page stays in its floating window until you choose **Return to Tab**, or close the window it came from.

- The page itself moves into a small floating window, so it keeps playing, and you can click and type in it.
- The window looks like Little Arx: the same bar along its top, with Back and Forward and the page's address. **Return to Tab** (the same icon as PiP's) sits where the window's close buttons would be, and puts the page back in its tab without reloading it.
- The address starts with the tab's space, shown as its shortcut: **⌃1**, **⌃2** and so on, counting from the left of the space switcher. Rest the pointer on it to see the space's name. Click it to list your spaces, and choose one to move the tab there. Or press :keys{combo="⌘ 1"} to :keys{combo="⌘ 9"} while the floating window is in front: :keys{combo="⌘ 2"} moves the tab to your second space. The window's sidebar shows the tab in its new space, the page keeps floating, and **Edit → Undo** puts the tab back.
- A space of another profile asks first, because the page reloads there and its sign-ins don't carry over: you may need to sign in again. **Move** reopens the page in that profile's space, and the new tab floats in the same window; **Cancel** or :keys{combo="Esc"} leaves everything as it was.
- The site button beside the address opens the site menu too: Share, your extensions, the site's permissions, content blocking, zoom, devices and hidden elements, and Clear Cache, Clear Cookies, Manage Extensions and All Site Settings in its **…** menu. A floating page leaves out the Capture Area, Capture Page, Picture in Picture, Reading Mode, Open in WebKit, Hide Element and Dark page buttons, adding an extension, and Translate; return the page to its tab to use them. The menu can reach past the window's edges. Little Arx floating as Picture in Picture has the same menu.
- A private window's page, a Peek's and a Little Arx page have no space to move, so their address keeps the profile button.
- The window stays on top, over full-screen apps, and follows you to every desktop. Drag the bar along its top to move it, from anywhere on it, the address and the buttons included (a drag never presses them), and let go or throw it: it settles in the corner it's heading for, on whichever display it lands. Drag an edge to resize it. Arx remembers the corner, and the size for each site.
- It belongs to the window it came from: closing that window closes Picture in Picture too.
- The tab says "Playing in Picture in Picture" while its page floats, with **Return to Tab**.

One page floats at a time. Private windows can float a page too. In Little Arx, the Little Arx window itself turns into Picture in Picture and back; see [Little Arx](/tabs/little-arx#picture-in-picture).

## Cast to a TV

Arx can show a tab, or a site's own video, on a Chromecast or a TV with Chromecast built in, on the same network as your Mac. It uses the same Cast technology as Chrome.

Open the Cast picker in any of these ways:

- Choose **File → Cast…**, or type **Cast** in the command bar.
- Right-click an empty part of the page and choose **Cast…**.
- Hover the player at the bottom of the sidebar and click its Cast button, to cast the tab that's playing.
- Click a site's own Cast button, such as YouTube's.

The picker lists the devices Arx finds. Click one to cast:

- If the site has its own way to cast, such as a YouTube video, the TV plays it itself, and you can keep using the tab. Right-click the device and choose **Cast Tab Instead** to show the whole tab.
- Otherwise the TV shows the tab as you see it.

A device that's casting says what it casts, with **Stop** beside it. Open the picker again to stop, or to cast something else to the same device. **Done** closes the picker without casting.

The first time you open the picker, macOS asks whether Arx may find devices on your local network. If you say no, the picker says so; turn on Arx in **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Local Network**. Arx looks for devices only after you first open the picker.

Private windows can cast too, as in Chrome.
