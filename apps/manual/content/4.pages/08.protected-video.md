Netflix, Disney+, Max, Prime Video, Spotify, Apple TV+, Apple Music and other services protect their video and music with DRM. Arx's Chromium pages can't play it, so Arx shows these sites in **WebKit pages**: the same engine as Safari, with macOS's FairPlay. They play at Safari's quality, including 1080p, 4K and HDR where the service and your Mac offer them, with hardware decoding that's easier on the battery.

A WebKit page is an ordinary tab. It sits in the sidebar, keeps its history, goes in your browsing history and follows your settings. Its icon has a small Safari compass in the bottom right corner, in the sidebar's tab list and on Favorites alike.

## Which sites open in WebKit

**Settings → Advanced → WebKit → WebKit sites** has the list. It starts with the main streaming services: Netflix, Disney+, Max and HBO Max, Prime Video, Hulu, Apple TV+, Apple Music, Spotify's player (`open.spotify.com`), Peacock, Paramount+, Crunchyroll and Hotstar.

- A site matches its https pages and all its subdomains: `netflix.com` covers `www.netflix.com`.
- **Add Site…** opens a form: type a site such as `example.com` and click **Add** (:keys{combo="↩"}). Click a listed site to change it in the same form, where **Remove** takes it off the list. The **–** button removes it too.
- **Restore Defaults** brings back the built-in sites and keeps the ones you added.
- When an Arx update adds a streaming service to the built-in sites, it joins your list too. A built-in site you took off the list stays off.
- **Open listed sites in WebKit** turns the whole thing off. Pages already in WebKit stay there until you go to another address.

Prime Video's pages on amazon.com aren't listed, because amazon.com is a shop too. Add it yourself if you watch there.

### Netflix

Netflix opens in a WebKit page, without asking, however you get there: typing its address, a bookmark, a link from another site, a tab Arx restores after a relaunch, [Peek](/tabs/peek) or [Little Arx](/tabs/little-arx). Its sign-in, browsing, account and player pages are all on `www.netflix.com`, so the `netflix.com` entry covers them. Netflix's other addresses, such as `netflix.net` or `http://netflix.com`, send you on to `www.netflix.com`, and the page moves to WebKit as it gets there.

To keep Netflix in Chromium, turn off the **Open in WebKit** button in its [site menu](/pages/site-menu), or remove it from the list. It stays in Chromium until you turn the button back on or click **Restore Defaults**. Its video doesn't play there: Arx's Chromium has no FairPlay for Netflix.

## Spotify in a Chromium tab

Spotify's player (`open.spotify.com`) stays in an ordinary Chromium tab. Arx's FairPlay helper plays its music, the same FairPlay that WebKit pages use. So the tab keeps your extensions, password autofill and assistants. Spotify's own buttons, the keyboard's media keys and the Now Playing controls in Control Center all work. Muting the tab mutes the music.

- If a song can't play this way, Arx moves the tab to a WebKit page. Spotify then opens in WebKit until you quit.
- Only one Spotify tab plays at a time. Starting a song in another tab pauses the first one, as Spotify does across devices.
- Spotify stays in Chromium even though it's on the WebKit list.
- Turning off **FairPlay in Chromium tabs** in **Settings → Advanced → Feature Flags** (the `ARX_FAIRPLAY_IN_CHROMIUM` feature flag) and relaunching Arx sends Spotify back to WebKit pages.

Netflix, Disney+ and the other video services still play in WebKit pages.

## Protected media settings

**Settings → Advanced → WebKit** has two settings next to the list:

- **Protected media** chooses how listed sites play.
  - **FairPlay in the tab** (the default) plays Spotify in its own tab, as above, and opens the other listed sites in WebKit pages. Services give FairPlay their best quality.
  - **Open in a WebKit page** opens every listed site, Spotify included, in a WebKit page.
- **Widevine answer** (on) tells sites that check for Widevine that it's there, as Chrome does. Some stores and checkouts refuse a browser without it, such as Apple's online store. Arx has no Widevine and never downloads it: a page that tries to use it is refused, and protected video and music always play with FairPlay. The change applies the next time Arx opens.

Assistants can read and change both settings.

## Moving a page

A page goes to WebKit when you:

- open or type the address of a listed site,
- click a link to one on another site,
- turn on **Open in WebKit** in its [site menu](/pages/site-menu),
- choose **Open in WebKit** when Arx asks, or
- reach a listed site any other way: a link within the same site (such as **Open Web Player** on spotify.com), a redirect, or the page's own script. The page moves as soon as it shows the listed site's address, or when it starts protected video or music there.

Before the page moves, Arx copies that site's cookies from the profile, so you stay signed in. **Back**, or a [swipe back](/tabs/gestures), from the first WebKit page opens the page you came from again. **Forward** from there shows the WebKit page again as you left it, with its own back and forward history. If that page is on a listed site, it stays in Chromium until you go to another site. So does a page that stayed because of a form you haven't sent. Typing the address of a site that isn't listed moves the tab back to Chromium; **Back** from there returns to the WebKit page, and **Forward**, or a swipe forward, from the WebKit page comes back. The Back and Forward menus list the pages on both sides. Links on a WebKit page stay in WebKit, and so do the tabs it opens.

## When a page asks for protected video

If a page that isn't listed starts protected video or music and can't play it, Arx asks: **Play example.com in WebKit?** A page that only checks what your browser supports isn't asked about: Apple's online store at checkout, or a site's bot check, which asks about every kind of protection as it would ask Chrome. A page that asks only for protection Arx's Chromium doesn't have, such as PlayReady, is asked about after a few seconds.

- **Open in WebKit** adds the site to the list and moves the page.
- **Stay on Page** leaves it in Chromium. Check **Don't ask again for this site** first to stop the question for that site.

A listed site's page that's still in Chromium moves to WebKit without asking.

Netflix, Disney+ and the other built-in streaming services can't play their video in a Chromium tab at all. A page of one of them that stayed in Chromium after **Back**, or that you took off the list, stays there until its video fails to start; then Arx asks before moving it.

## When WebKit pages are off

With **Open listed sites in WebKit** turned off, a listed site that starts protected video or music can't play. Arx tells you so, and that turning WebKit pages on in **Settings → Advanced → WebKit** plays it in Arx.

- For Spotify, if the Spotify app is installed, **Open in Spotify** opens the same song, album, playlist or podcast in it.
- **OK** closes the message. Check **Don't ask again for this site** first to stop it for that site.

Sites that aren't listed get no message while WebKit pages are off.

Leave a page with a form you haven't sent and it asks first, as it would before closing. A WebKit page can't ask: closing it or leaving it doesn't warn about unsent forms.

## Each profile keeps its own

Each profile has its own WebKit storage, so a sign-in in one profile never shows in another. A private window's WebKit pages keep nothing after it closes. Clear Browsing Data and the site menu's Clear Cookies and Clear Cache clear WebKit pages' data too.

## Right-click menu

Right-click a WebKit page, or a video playing in it, for the same page menu as other tabs: **Back**, **Forward**, **Reload**, **Save Page As…**, **Print…**, **Share…** and **View Page Source**. Only what works on that page right now is listed. In full screen, **Exit Full Screen** comes first. Links, images and selected text get WebKit's own menu, as in Safari.

- **Save Page As…** saves the page as a **Web Archive (Safari)**, **Web Page, HTML Only** (the HTML the site sent) or a **PDF**.
- **Print…** opens the print dialog as Safari does, on Page Setup's paper.
- **View Page Source** opens the source in a new tab.
- With developer mode on, **Inspect Element** opens Safari's Web Inspector for the page.

Cast and Translate aren't in the menu: WebKit pages have neither.

## What WebKit pages don't have

WebKit pages don't have:

- extensions,
- password autofill,
- Chrome's site settings (the site menu's permissions and zoom),
- page translation,
- notifications,
- passkeys, until Apple approves Arx for them,
- assistants and MCP tools: agents can't read or act on a WebKit page,
- Arx's Downloads list: a file a WebKit page downloads goes straight into your Downloads folder, and the Finder shows it.

Content blocking works: WebKit pages block with the same filter lists as uBlock Origin, and follow **Content blocking**, **Block cookie banners** and each site's own switch in the site menu. Find in page works, but says only whether there's a match.

Picture in Picture and full screen are WebKit's own, as in Safari.
