## Security updates

A certificate can be revoked long after a browser was built: its key leaked, or it was issued by mistake. Arx keeps three pieces of security data current so that it notices:

- **The certificate revocation list**: certificates revoked since this version of Arx was built.
- **Certificate Transparency and root store data**: the public logs a certificate must appear in, and the certificate authorities Arx trusts.
- **Certificate error help**: the explanations on certificate error pages.

Arx asks Google's component service (`update.googleapis.com`) for them a minute after it opens, then every five hours while it runs. Nothing else is downloaded this way.

- Each check names only these three components and their versions, Arx's Chromium version, the macOS version and the processor type. It carries no identifier for your Mac, no usage counts and no cookies.
- Arx sends no install or update reports.
- Every answer is signed, and every download is checked against its component's signature before Arx uses it.

With [developer mode](/developers/developer-mode#security-data) on, **Settings → Developer** shows the version of each one that Arx is using.

## The key to your passwords and cookies

Arx encrypts saved passwords, cookies and autofill data with a key it keeps in your login keychain, named **Arx Storage Key**. If that key were replaced, none of them could be read again. So Arx never replaces it on its own: when it can't read the key, it stops before loading any profile and tells you why.

- **Your login keychain is locked.** Unlock it in Keychain Access, then click **Try Again**.
- **macOS didn't let Arx use the key**, because you clicked Deny or the prompt was closed. Click **Try Again** and choose **Always Allow**.
- **You changed your Mac's login password** and your login keychain still uses the old one. Unlock it with the old password, or update its password in Keychain Access, then click **Try Again**.
- **The key is missing** from your login keychain while Arx has saved data. Put it back if you moved or deleted it, for example from a Time Machine backup, and click **Try Again**. **Start Over…** makes a new key instead: Arx asks once more, then opens without your saved passwords, and websites sign you out.

**Quit Arx** leaves everything as it was. On a brand-new Mac or account, with nothing saved yet, Arx creates its key without asking. A [headless run](/developers/headless) can't ask: it exits with `70` and writes `ARX_SAFE_STORAGE_UNAVAILABLE` with macOS's error code to stderr.

Arx also clears decrypted passwords from memory as soon as it's done with them.

## Phishing and malware

Arx blocks known phishing and malware sites with three of [uBlock Origin](/pages/content-blocking)'s lists, all on by default:

- **Phishing URL Blocklist**: sites that imitate a bank, a shop or a sign-in page to take your password.
- **Online Malicious URL Blocklist** (URLhaus): addresses that serve malware.
- **uBlock filters – Badware risks**: sites that push fake downloads, scams and unwanted software.

uBlock Origin keeps them up to date on its own, the phishing and malware lists about twice a day, from the people who publish them. Those servers see your Mac's IP address when a list updates, and nothing about the pages you visit. Arx checks each page against the copy on your Mac.

A blocked site shows uBlock Origin's page with the list that matched. **Go back** leaves; **Proceed** opens the site anyway. To stop using one of these lists, open uBlock Origin's dashboard and clear it under **Filter lists**. With content blocking off, these lists are off too.

Arx doesn't use Google Safe Browsing, the same as Helium and Ardia, the browsers it's built from. The lists catch sites someone has already reported, which takes a few hours for a new phishing site, so check the address before you sign in anywhere.

### Downloads

Arx doesn't warn you before it downloads a file type that can run code on your Mac, such as `.dmg`, `.pkg`, `.app` or `.command`. Helium and Ardia don't either. uBlock Origin's lists still block downloads from addresses on them, and macOS checks an app or installer you downloaded with Gatekeeper the first time you open it.

## iCloud sync

[Sync](/profiles/sync) is off until you turn it on. Then Arx keeps what you chose to sync in your own iCloud account's private database, which only your Apple Account can read. It goes from your Mac to Apple's iCloud servers and back, never through Arx's, and the Arx team can't see it. Arx asks CloudKit to encrypt every field it stores. Whether iCloud also encrypts it end to end depends on whether you turned on Advanced Data Protection for your Apple Account.

Cookies, sign-ins, passwords, open tabs, windows and assistant grants never sync. History and Tab Archive sync only if you turn them on.

## Member number

Your identity card's [member number](/start/settings#member-number) is the one thing Arx gets from its own server, `api.arxbrowser.net`, and only when you click **Get your member number**.

- Arx asks Apple only to confirm your account: no name, no email.
- Apple's sign-in opens in a small private window with a private profile of its own, which Arx discards when the window closes, so no Apple cookie stays in Arx.
- It sends the sign-in's token to Arx's server. With Apple, the token also passes through that server on its way back to Arx, because Apple returns it only to a web address; nothing is kept from that step. The server checks it with Apple and keeps a hash of the account's ID with its number and the day it first signed in. It doesn't keep the token, your name, your email or your IP address.
- With the token, Arx sends the day it was installed on this Mac: only the day, with no time and nothing about the Mac. The server keeps the earliest such day any Mac sent for your account, starting from the day you first signed in, and answers with it, so your card shows the same **Member since** on every Mac. A day before Arx existed or in the future is ignored.
- Arx keeps the number and the days on your Mac. It keeps nothing else from the sign-in.

Arx records when it was first installed on your Mac, for the card's **Member since**, with its other settings. It goes to the server only as that day, and only when you claim a member number. It isn't synced through iCloud.

## Crash reports and problem reports

Arx sends nothing about how you use it: no usage statistics, no analytics and no crash reports. When Arx crashes, it keeps a short report on your Mac for 30 days, with no web addresses, nothing from your pages and no identifier for you or your Mac. It stays on your Mac.

**Help → Report a Problem…** opens a GitHub issue for the Arx team in a new tab, filled in with what you wrote and the versions of Arx, its engine and macOS. Nothing is sent until you submit the issue yourself, and the diagnostics file, with recent crash reports, goes along only if you attach it. See [Crash reports and problem reports](/help/crash-reports).

**Help → Send Feedback…** sends a report privately to Arx's maintainer when you click **Send**: what you wrote, your email if you give one, the files you attach, the versions of Arx and macOS, and, with **Include diagnostics** on, the same diagnostics as Report a Problem. Arx's server passes it on and keeps none of it. See [Send feedback](/help/send-feedback).
