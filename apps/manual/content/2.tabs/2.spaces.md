Work, home, a side project. Each space keeps its own pinned tabs and open tabs, so switching changes the whole sidebar at once. Favorites stay the same in every space of a profile.

::

## Switch spaces

- **Swipe** horizontally with two fingers over the sidebar. All your spaces sit on one track, with the Library next to the space you opened it from. One swipe moves one space, however far your fingers go; swipe again for the next. As the sidebar slides, the window's background turns from one space's theme to the next along with it. The address bar stays where it is and shows the space you land on; Favorites stay too while both spaces share a profile, and slide with the tabs when the profile changes.
- **Click** a space in the switcher at the bottom of the sidebar. Its icons sit centered between the Library button and **+**; the one under the pointer, and **+**, sit on a light tile, a little darker while you click. As the sidebar narrows, the gaps close up. When the icons no longer fit, every other space becomes a dot and only the current space keeps its icon. With even more spaces, scroll the row sideways. Scrolling the row never switches spaces. Rest the pointer on a dot to see that space's icon in its place (the current space turns into a dot meanwhile); keep it still there for a second to see a hint with the space's name, its profile and its shortcut. While the hint is up, it follows the pointer to the next space straight away; move off the switcher and the next hint waits a second again.
- **Press** :keys{combo="⌃ 1"} to :keys{combo="⌃ 9"} for the space in that place of the switcher, counting from the left. A number with no space does nothing. They're also in the **Spaces** menu, and you can change them in [Settings → Shortcuts](/start/keyboard-shortcuts).
- **Type** its name in the command bar and pick **Switch to Space**.
- **Press** :keys{combo="⌥ ⌘ →"} for **Next Space** and :keys{combo="⌥ ⌘ ←"} for **Previous Space**. Past the last space they go round to the first, and back. You can change both in [Settings → Shortcuts](/start/keyboard-shortcuts).

## Make a space

Choose **New Space** from the Spaces menu, the Library's menu or the command bar.

Or swipe past your last space. A round **+** grows at the sidebar's edge as you pull; once it fills with the accent color, let go and the sidebar moves on to **Create a Space**. A short swipe just springs back. Name the space, pick its profile (the current space's, to begin with) and, if you like, **Choose a Theme**. **Create Space** or :keys{combo="↩"} makes it and shows it; **Cancel**, :keys{combo="esc"} or a swipe back returns to your last space. A mouse's sideways scroll steps there too. Private windows don't offer it.

Right-click a space for:

- **Change Space Icon…**: any SF Symbol or emoji, from a searchable picker. A space without one shows a small dot. The trash button beside the picker's search field takes the icon off again.
- **Rename Space…**
- **Appearance**: the space's [theme](#space-themes), whether it uses [Match site](/start/settings#match-site) to take the color of the page it shows, and its own [accent color](/start/settings#accent-color).
- **Set Profile**: move the space to another profile.
- **New Folder**, **Manage Spaces…** and **Delete Space**.

Deleting a space asks first, and any page can refuse to close. When another Mac syncs it with iCloud, it asks whether to [delete it from this Mac only or remove it from iCloud](/profiles/sync#deleting-a-profile-or-space-another-mac-syncs). A space another Mac removed from iCloud shows a warning triangle in the space switcher; its menu has **Remove Here Too** and **Keep**. Your last space can't be deleted. For a minute afterwards, :keys{combo="⌘ Z"} in any window brings it back with its tabs and folders.

## Space themes

**Choose a Theme** while creating a space, or **Appearance** on a space's menu, opens one panel. The space's sidebar changes as you go. To see what the panel covers, drag it by the handle along its top edge: it stays open where you leave it until you close it with :keys{combo="Esc"}, a click elsewhere in the window, or the same button again, and opens beside the button again next time.

- **Color field.** The dotted field shows the space in its colors. Each color is a dot inside the circle the field holds: going round the circle changes the hue, and the color is strongest at the centre and fades towards the edge. Click the empty field to place the first color. **+** adds a color, up to three, which the sidebar blends as a gradient: the second sits opposite the first, and with a third the two others sit either side of that opposite point. The larger dot is the main one. Drag it and the others turn and move with it, keeping their places around the circle; drag past the edge and the dots slide along it. Press a smaller dot and it becomes the main one where it stands, so you can drag on from there. Click elsewhere in the field to move the main dot there. Every dot grows a little while you hold one. **−** removes a color; removing the last one empties the field.
- **Automatic, light or dark.** The three buttons at the top of the field. Automatic follows the profile and your Mac.
- **Preset colors.** The row under the field replaces the colors with a preset. The arrows page between single colors, gradients of three neighbouring colors, and dark colors, which also switch the space to dark.
- **Intensity.** The wave slider sets how much color every dot has at once, which moves the dots towards the centre or the edge together. Its knob shows once the space has a color of its own. It also sets how much of your desktop shows through the sidebar and the frame around the page, blurred: low intensity lets the most through, and from about the slider's middle on the window is solid. The page itself is always solid. With **Reduce Transparency** or **Increase Contrast** on in System Settings → Accessibility → Display, in full screen, and while [Match site](/start/settings#match-site) colors the window, it stays solid.
- **Texture.** Click the dial's inner circle to go through Noise, Jeans, Paper, Textile (fine diagonal woven stripes) and Clear (no texture). Drag around its ring of dots to make the texture stronger or fainter. The texture covers the window's background only: hover previews, the command bar and other panels over the window keep their plain glass.

A new space uses its profile's colors and texture until you choose its own. Remove the last dot to go back to the profile's colors, or right-click the dial and choose **Use the Profile's Texture**. **Use Profile** at the bottom resets the whole theme.

Everything works from the keyboard and VoiceOver. Tab to a dot and use the arrow keys: left and right turn every dot's hue together, up and down change their intensity, and Delete removes a color. The wave slider and the dial take the arrow keys too, and Space on the dial moves to the next texture. VoiceOver reads each dot's color and intensity, adjusts its hue, and offers More Intense, Less Intense, Make Main Color and Remove Color as actions.

Settings › Appearance & Sound and each profile in Settings › Profiles show the same panel, in the page. See [Settings](/start/settings#window-background).

## Order and profiles

Drag a space's icon or dot in the switcher to reorder spaces: it lifts and follows the pointer while the others make room, or drag them in **Manage Spaces…** (the Library's Spaces section). Both show the same order, and Arx keeps it. Every space belongs to one [profile](/profiles/profiles). Moving a space to another profile reopens its tabs there with that profile's cookies; its Favorites stay with the old profile.

## Moving tabs between spaces

Drag a tab onto another space in the switcher, or into its sidebar, or use **Move Items…** (:keys{combo="⇧ ⌘ M"}). A page floating in [Picture in Picture](/pages/media#page-in-picture-in-picture) moves from the space button in its address, or with :keys{combo="⌘ 1"} to :keys{combo="⌘ 9"} while it's in front. Within a profile the tab moves as it is, page and all.

A space of another profile asks first, whichever way you move. It says what happens: the page reloads in that profile, and its sign-ins don't carry over, so you may need to sign in again. **Move** goes ahead; **Cancel** or :keys{combo="Esc"} leaves the tab where it was. Moving a whole space to another profile asks the same way.

## Windows

Every window shows your spaces; a window doesn't own its tabs, so closing one loses nothing, and **File → Reopen Closed Window** brings it back on the space and tab it showed. **Window → Keep Window on Top** keeps the front window above other apps' windows until you choose it again. It isn't remembered after you quit.
