## Make a split

A split holds two to four tabs. There are a few ways to build one:

- **Drop a tab onto another tab** in the sidebar, open or pinned. The two become a split. Drop a tab onto a split's row to add it to that split.
- **Drop a tab onto the page.** Each edge of the page owns the triangle between its two corners and the middle of the page, so wherever the pointer is, it picks an edge and you don't have to aim for it. The left and right triangles add a pane on that side; the top and bottom ones add a pane above or below, which stacks the split. The half of the page the new pane will take lights up.
- **Choose Add a pane** from a tab's menu, the View menu, the Library's Split View menu or the command bar. The new pane opens the command bar so you can pick its page; dismiss it and the pane closes again.
- **Choose Add Tab…** from a split row's menu.

A fifth tab is refused, and so is a pane the window has no room for: every pane is at least 280 points wide (or tall, when stacked). When a drop isn't allowed, the edge or row shows why and nothing changes. If you let go anyway, a small toast at the top right of the window says why. It goes away by itself after a couple of seconds and stays while the pointer is over it.

Any tab can join a split, however you add it: dropped on a tab or a split's row in the sidebar, dropped on the page, or added from a menu. A split's tabs stay together in one place in the sidebar, so a tab that joins moves next to the tab it joins:

- An open tab dropped on a pinned tab is pinned, and the two make a pinned split.
- A pinned tab dropped on an open tab is unpinned, and they make an open split.
- A pinned tab dropped on another pinned tab makes a pinned split, as two open tabs do.
- A tab dropped on a tab in a folder moves into the folder, and the split stays there.

Undo puts the tab back where it was. In the Spaces overview, a tab from another space joins too and moves to the split's space.

To split the tab you're on with another one, press the other tab in the sidebar and drag it onto the page without letting go. Pressing it shows it at once; dropping it on an edge makes a split of it and the tab you were on before, with the dragged tab on that edge.

A [chat tab](/command-bar/chat#open-a-chat-in-a-tab) joins a split like any other tab, so you can keep a chat next to the page it's about. Right after **Open in Tab**, the chat shows where the page was: drag the chat tab onto the page's edge and the chat and that page become a split.

## Pinned splits

A pinned split is one row in Pinned or in a folder, like any pinned tab, and Arx keeps it as it keeps pinned tabs. To move a whole split, drag its row, or choose **Pin Split View**, **Unpin Split View** or **Move to…** from its menu. Unpinning a split unpins every tab in it, and it stays a split; pinning it does the same the other way.

## Favorites in a split

A Favorite can join a split too: drop it on a tab, onto the page, or pick it for a split's empty pane. It stays in the Favorites grid. While the split shows, the Favorite's tile stays highlighted, along with the other tabs' rows. The sidebar's row for the split lists only its other tabs: a split of a Favorite and one open tab shows that tab's own row, and a split of a Favorite and two open tabs shows one split row of the two. Click the Favorite or any of its tabs to show the split again.

## Drag a tab out of the window

Drop a tab from the sidebar anywhere outside Arx's windows, such as on the desktop, and it moves into a [Little Arx](/tabs/little-arx) window that opens where you let go. The page keeps going as it was, and the tab leaves the sidebar; if it was in a split, it leaves the split. A chat tab moves the same way and shows its chat in Little Arx.

Little Arx stays in the tab's space and profile. Pinned tabs, Favorites and tabs in private windows stay where they are.

## The split's row

A split shows as one row in the sidebar, with a segment for each tab: its icon and title. The row looks the same in the open tabs, in Pinned and in a folder. Click a segment to show the split with that pane focused. Click any other tab and the split stays in the sidebar for later.

Hover a segment for its button. On an open tab, × closes the tab. On a pinned tab, × resets it to its saved page, as on any pinned tab's row; once it's there, the button turns into −, which separates it from the split and leaves it pinned.

Each pane has its own back, forward, address, link, [site menu](/pages/site-menu) and close buttons, and the focused pane has an accent outline. The link button copies that pane's address, and the site menu button focuses the pane and opens its site menu. A pane's address is its whole URL without `https://`, such as `rezka.ag/ua/films/drama/…`: the site always shows, and the rest is cut off at the end when the pane is narrow. Click it to edit that pane's address in the command bar, as :keys{combo="⌘ L"} does for the focused pane.

Click anywhere in a pane to focus it: its header, its page or an empty pane. The click still does what it would on the page, so clicking a link in the other pane focuses that pane and follows the link.

## Resize

Panes start equal, and adding a tab makes them equal again. Drag the divider between two panes to give one more room than the other; the panes further away don't move. Double-click a divider to make every pane the same size again.

## The split's menu

Right-click a split's row for:

| Item | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Share Split View… | Shares the links of every tab in the split |
| Separate All Tabs | Ends the split. Its tabs stay in the sidebar as separate tabs. |
| Add Tab… | Adds an empty pane and opens the command bar for it |
| Convert to Vertical (or Horizontal) Split View | Stacks the panes, or puts them side by side |
| Pin Split View (or Unpin Split View) | Moves the whole split to Pinned, or to the open tabs. It stays a split. |
| Move to… | Moves the whole split to another space or folder |
| Tab Options | A menu for each tab: Separate from Split View, Share…, Mute, Copy Link, Copy Link as Markdown, Peek, Duplicate, Move, and Close (Reset for a pinned tab away from its saved page) |
| Change Icon… | Puts a symbol or emoji at the start of the row |
| Rename… | Names the split. The row then shows the name and the tabs' icons. |
| Duplicate | Makes a new split with a copy of each tab, right after it. A pinned split's copies are pinned. |

Drag any segment to move the whole split in the sidebar: to another place in the list, into Pinned, into a folder or back to the open tabs. To take one tab out, use **Separate from Split View**, drag its segment onto the page, or close its pane.

## Work in a split

| Command | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Focus other pane | Moves the focus to the next pane |
| Swap panes | Reverses the order of the panes |
| Change split orientation | Side by side or stacked |
| Close pane | Closes the active pane and takes its tab out of the split. The tab stays in the sidebar; a pinned tab stays as it is, without being reset. |

None of these has a default key; you can add them in [Settings → Shortcuts](/start/keyboard-shortcuts).

Arx remembers each split's tabs, their order, the orientation and the pane sizes, and brings them back when you open it again.

Assistants can make a split of two to four tabs, side by side or stacked, with [`browser_windows`](/assistants/tools).
