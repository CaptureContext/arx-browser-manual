## Back and forward

Swipe horizontally with two fingers on a page. A glass arrow follows your fingers; let go past the line and Arx goes back or forward one page. Let go early and nothing happens.

It works on any page you can see, without clicking it first: a window behind another app, [Little Arx](/tabs/little-arx) and a page floating in [Picture in Picture](/pages/media#page-in-picture-in-picture) too. A Magic Mouse swipe with one finger shows the same arrow.

Pages that play protected video and music in WebKit, such as Netflix, swipe the same way, with the same arrow and history list, though there the list shows a globe in place of each site's icon. A page that scrolls sideways itself, like a carousel or a map, keeps the swipe until it reaches its edge. Swiping back from the first WebKit page returns to the page you came from, and the list shows that page last.

Swiping on WebKit pages is being tested and is on in the beta. Turning off **Swipe on WebKit pages** in **Settings → Advanced → Feature Flags** (the `ARX_WEBKIT_SWIPE` feature flag) and relaunching Arx gives WebKit pages Safari's own swipe, which can't return to the page you came from.

## Your whole history

Push further and hold for a moment. A list of your history opens beside the arrow, with each page's icon, title and site, and the page nearest to this one level with the arrow. Down is always back and up is always forward: swiping back, the list hangs down from the arrow and you move your fingers down for older pages; swiping forward, it rises from the arrow and you move them up for newer ones. Each step gives a tick of haptic feedback, and each takes a deliberate move of your fingers, so the selection doesn't run ahead of you. Let go to open the selected page. The selected page always stays level with the arrow: the list shows five pages at a time and scrolls through longer histories.

The Back and Forward buttons also show your history as a menu.

## Between spaces

Swipe across the sidebar to move between [spaces](/tabs/spaces).

Three- and four-finger swipes keep macOS's own behavior.
