The sidebar runs down the side of the window, the left by default, and holds everything in the current space. **Settings → Appearance & Sound → Sidebar → Position** puts it on the right; the window buttons move with it.

::preview{scene="sidebar-folders" caption="Drag a tab into a pinned folder, rename the folder, nest a new folder inside it, then Clear the unpinned tabs."}
::

## Three sections

| Section | What lives there |
| --- | --- |
| **Favorites** | A grid of tiles for the sites you use everywhere. They belong to the profile, so they show in every space of it. |
| **Pinned** | Pinned tabs and folders. They keep their place until you move them. |
| **Unpinned tabs** | Everything else you open. A tab from the **New Tab** button, :keys{combo="⌘ T"} or the command bar starts at the top, as in Arc; [choose where](/start/settings#where-new-tabs-open). **Clear** sends them all to the [Tab Archive](/tabs/archive). |

Drag a tab into Favorites or Pinned to keep it there, or press :keys{combo="⌘ B"} to pin the current tab.

## Tabs coming and going

Tabs move into place instead of jumping, as in Arc. A new tab slides in where it opens while the tabs below make room: from the **New Tab** button or :keys{combo="⌘ T"} at the top, and from a link, **Duplicate** or a Peek under the tab it came from. A tab you close or archive folds away and the tabs below close up. Moving a tab between Favorites, Pinned, a folder and the unpinned tabs, reordering, and opening or closing a folder move the same way, and a Favorite grows into its tile as the others make room.

The motion is short, a third of a second. Nothing moves when you switch spaces or when a window opens and fills its sidebar, and with **Reduce motion** on in **System Settings → Accessibility → Display**, tabs appear and disappear at once.

::preview{scene="sidebar-motion"}

Arx is testing the motion. To have tabs change at once, as before, turn its feature flag off and quit and reopen Arx:

```bash
defaults write dev.capturecontext.arx ARX_SIDEBAR_ROW_ANIMATIONS -string off
```

To bring the motion back:

```bash
defaults delete dev.capturecontext.arx ARX_SIDEBAR_ROW_ANIMATIONS
```

## Favorites

Favorites fill the sidebar's width. Tiles share a row equally: one Favorite is a full-width tile, two are halves, and a row holds up to four before the next row starts. Tiles are flat and lightly tinted, like the address bar above them, and the open Favorite takes the selected tab's brighter fill with a thin outline in its site's color.

- **Add one**: drag a tab up into the grid. The other tiles move aside to open a space for it, and the dragged tab turns into a tile glowing in its site's color. Let go to drop it in.
- **Reorder**: drag a tile to a new place in the grid.
- **Remove one**: drag a tile down into the tab list. It turns back into a row, and dropping it there makes it an ordinary tab again. **Remove Favorite** in its menu does the same.
- **No Favorites yet**: a dashed **Drag to add Favorites** card shows at the top of the sidebar. Drag a tab onto it, and it lights up in the accent color without moving anything under it; let go to add the tab. Click its × to hide it until you close the window.

Site colors come from a built-in list of well-known sites, such as YouTube's red, Google and Gmail's four colors, and GitHub's black (white in Dark Mode). Any other site uses the main color of its icon, or the accent color when the icon has none.

Favorites belong to a profile. Another profile never shows them.

## Site icons

Tabs and Favorites show their site's icon even when the page isn't loaded, such as after you quit and reopen Arx or once a tab is unloaded. Arx remembers the last icon each page showed and updates it whenever the page loads. A site with no icon shows a small soft gray square in its place.

A Favorite or pinned tab shows the icon of the page it's on, like any tab. If you follow a link to another site from it, its icon is that site's while you're there. When you close it, it goes back to its saved page and shows that page's icon again. A custom icon from **Change Icon…** stays whichever page is open.

A Favorite or pinned tab that has never loaded, such as one you just brought over from another browser, gets its icon from the site: Arx asks the site's home page which icon it uses, or tries its standard icon, without sending cookies, and never opens the saved page itself. It asks only once the tab is on screen: a tab in another space when you switch to that space, a tab in a folder when you open the folder or preview it. It asks a few sites at a time, so reopening Arx doesn't contact every site at once. Once the page loads, its own icon replaces that one. To stop Arx from asking sites, turn off **Fetch icons for tabs that haven't loaded** in **Settings → General**; such tabs then show the gray square until you open them. Private windows never ask.

Each profile keeps its own icons. Deleting a profile deletes its icons too.

## Unloaded tabs

To save memory, Arx unloads a tab you haven't looked at for a while: after 3 hours by default, or once it's been hidden for a minute when your Mac runs critically low on memory. Change how long in **Settings → General → Tabs → Unload inactive tabs**, or both in [Settings → Advanced → Memory](/start/settings#memory). An unloaded tab keeps its place, title, icon and back and forward history. Its row looks faded in the sidebar, and it loads again as soon as you open it. A regular tab left alone for 6 hours then moves to the [Tab Archive](/tabs/archive#automatic-archiving), unless you choose otherwise.

Arx keeps these loaded:

- Tabs you can see, in any window, Peek or Little Arx, and each space's current tab.
- Tabs playing sound or in Picture in Picture, using your camera, microphone or screen, or connected to a USB or Bluetooth device.
- Pages where you've typed into a form, and PDFs.
- Tabs with DevTools open, and tabs an assistant is working in.
- Pages connected to their server for live updates, such as chat, through a WebSocket or WebTransport connection. Arx also keeps these running when they're in the background, so they stay connected and see new messages straight away.
- Sites you keep loaded, and pinned tabs and Favorites if you choose (see below).
- Unless memory runs critically low: sites that may send you notifications, and pages that change their title or icon in the background, such as mail and chat.

To keep a site loaded, such as a music player or a dashboard, Control-click its tab or Favorite and choose **Always Keep This Site Loaded**. Its tabs in that profile then never unload, even when memory runs low, and the site's subdomains are kept too. Choose it again to stop. **Keep pinned tabs loaded**, and **Sites kept loaded**, which opens each profile's list, are in [Settings → Advanced](/start/settings#memory).

Pages also lose what they kept only in memory, such as a half-played video's position, so a page may reload a little differently from how you left it.

### Closing a window

Closing a window unloads the tabs it showed that no other open window shows: its space's tabs and Favorites, or every tab when it's the last window. Arx keeps running, and the tabs load again when a window shows them. A page you've typed into that would warn you before you leave it asks first, while the window is still open: **Leave** closes the window and unloads the page with the others, and **Stay** keeps the window open.

## Links that open a new tab

**Open in New Tab** from a link's menu, :keys{combo="⌘ Click"} and a middle-click open the link in a new tab behind the page you're reading, so you stay where you are. It starts loading straight away, quietly in the background, and is ready when you open it.

Where the new tab goes depends on the tab the link is in:

- From a tab among your unpinned tabs, it goes right below that tab, every time. A second link from the same tab goes between that tab and the first one, so the newest is nearest.
- From a Favorite or a pinned tab, in a folder or not, it goes to the top of the unpinned tabs. It never joins your Favorites, Pinned or the folder.

This holds whether the tab opens behind the page or comes forward, including links a page opens in a new tab itself. The [New tabs open](/start/settings#where-new-tabs-open) setting places only tabs you open with the **New Tab** button, :keys{combo="⌘ T"} and :keys{combo="⌘ K"}.

The same goes for every other tab made from a tab:

- **Duplicate Tab**, from the sidebar, the **Tab** menu, the command bar or :keys{combo="⌘ D"}, puts the copy right below the tab it copies.
- **Open as Tab** on a [Peek](/tabs/peek) puts the page right below the tab the Peek opened over.

From a Favorite or a pinned tab, the copy or the page goes to the top of the unpinned tabs instead.

To go to those tabs as they open, turn on **Settings → General → Tabs → When a link opens in a new tab, switch to it**. The tab then comes forward in the same place. :keys{combo="⇧ ⌘ Click"} does the opposite of the setting, either way.

A link the page itself opens in a new tab comes forward, as in other browsers. Links in Peek, Little Arx and private windows keep their own rules.

## Where a link goes

Resting the pointer on a link in a page shows its address in a small label at the page's bottom-left corner, without `https://` and shortened in the middle when it's long, moving to the bottom-right when the pointer is over that corner. Holding keys makes the label say what a click will do, with the address inside it, and it changes as you press and release them: :keys{combo="⌘"} **in a new tab behind this one**, :keys{combo="⇧ ⌘"} **in a new tab and go to it**, :keys{combo="⇧"} **in Peek** (or **in a new window**), :keys{combo="⌥ ⌘"} **in Little Arx**, :keys{combo="⌥"} **Download**. It follows your Links settings and Air Traffic Control, shows in Split View, Peek, Little Arx and private windows, and keeps nothing about the links you hover. **Settings → Links → Link Status → Show where links go** turns it off.

## Pinned tabs and Favorites keep a saved page

A pinned tab or Favorite remembers the page you saved. When you close it with :keys{combo="⌘ W"}, Arx unloads it and resets it to that page instead of deleting it. To save where it is now, choose **Update Saved URL** from its menu.

A pinned tab gives a quick shake in the sidebar when it's reset, wherever it is, in Pinned or in a folder. With **Reduce motion** on in System Settings › Accessibility › Display, its row lights up briefly instead.

## Closing the tab you're on

When you close the tab you're on, or reset the pinned tab or Favorite you're on, Arx goes back to the tab you were on before it, in the same space. It remembers one tab per window. If that tab is gone too, or it's in another space, no tab is selected, as before.

## Folders

- **New Folder** from the Tabs menu, a folder's menu (**New Subfolder**) or the command bar. Folders can hold folders.
- :keys{combo="⌘ Return"} on a folder opens its tabs; :keys{combo="⇧ ⌘ Return"} opens its subfolders' tabs too.
- **Ungroup Folder** (:keys{combo="⇧ ⌘ G"}) keeps the tabs and removes the folder.
- **Delete Folder and Tabs** (:keys{combo="Delete"}) closes everything inside, after each page agrees to close.

Renaming, reordering, ungrouping and deleting folders can be undone with :keys{combo="⌘ Z"}.

### A closed folder shows its current tab

When the tab you're on is inside a closed folder, the folder stays closed and shows just that tab beneath it, indented. Its icon shows **•••** to say it holds more. This happens however you got to the tab: the command bar, a link, History, :keys{combo="⌃ Tab"} or a split.

- Switch to a tab outside the folder, and the folder hides its tab again.
- Open the folder to see all its tabs as usual. Close a folder while you're on one of its tabs, and that tab stays showing.
- In folders inside folders, the tab shows under the outermost closed folder you can see.
- When the tab is part of a [split](/tabs/split-view), the folder shows the whole split.
- A closed tab group does the same with its current tab.

## Group open tabs

Related open tabs can share a group without pinning them. Mark them with :keys{combo="⌘ Click"} and choose **Group Tabs** from one's menu, or choose **Add Tab to New Group** on a single tab. The group shows as one row holding its tabs, like a folder among your open tabs.

- Click the group's row to fold it or open it. Press :keys{combo="Return"} on it, or choose **Rename…** from its menu, to rename it.
- Drop a tab on the group's row, or just under it, to add it. Drag a tab elsewhere to take it out. Dropping a tab on another tab still puts them side by side in [Split View](/tabs/split-view).
- **Pin Group** turns it into a pinned folder with the same name and tabs. **Ungroup Tabs** leaves the tabs where they are. **Archive Tabs** closes them all into the [Tab Archive](/tabs/archive) together.

Each of these can be undone with :keys{combo="⌘ Z"}.

## Hover previews

Rest the pointer on a tab, a Favorite or a folder for a second to see what's in it, without clicking. Hold :keys{combo="⇧"} as you move onto it, or press :keys{combo="⇧"} while the pointer is on it, and the preview opens at once.

Previews float beside the item as glass tiles over the page, with no panel around them.

- **A tab or Favorite** shows a picture of the page in one tile and, in the tile under it, its title, its site and when you last used it. If the page is loaded, the picture is live. If it isn't, the card shows the last picture Arx took of it in that profile, or else the preview image and description the site publishes for it. You can have a tab with no picture yet load out of sight to take one instead; see [Tabs that aren't loaded](#tabs-that-arent-loaded).
- **A folder**, open or closed, shows a search field named after it ("Search Reading") with a Close button, and under them every tab inside it, including its subfolders' tabs grouped under their folder names, each with its site and when you last used it. A Split View in the folder is one row naming both of its tabs. Type in the search field to filter by title, address or folder name, and click a tab to open it. The folder stays as it was. An empty folder says "No tabs in this folder" under its search field, and a search that finds nothing says "No matching tabs".

  To search by keyboard, rest the pointer on the folder and press :keys{combo="Tab"} to move into its search field. While you type, the first matching tab is selected. :keys{combo="↑"} and :keys{combo="↓"} move the selection, and so does the pointer. :keys{combo="Return"} opens the selected tab.
- **A Gmail Favorite** also lists, in a tile of its own, your unread mail: how many there are, and the latest five with sender and time. Gmail has to be open in that Favorite; if it isn't, the card says so, and hovering doesn't open it.
- **A Linear, Notion or Figma Favorite** also lists, in a tile of its own, the pages you opened there most recently in that profile: issues, projects, documents and views in Linear, pages in Notion, files in Figma. They come from the profile's history, so only pages you opened in Arx show.

After one preview closes, the next one opens straight away as you move along the sidebar. A tab's card closes as soon as the pointer leaves it. A folder's list stays open while you move into it. Once you've pressed :keys{combo="Tab"} into its search, clicked in it or typed, it stays open even when nothing matches ("No matching tabs"), until you open a tab, click Close, click elsewhere or press :keys{combo="Esc"}.

A window shows one preview at a time. Moving onto another tab, Favorite or folder replaces the open preview at once, with no moment where both show. A preview you're using (its search has the keyboard or text in it, or the pointer is on it) stays while the pointer only brushes past other rows; resting on another row for a second, or holding :keys{combo="⇧"} on it, opens that row's preview in its place.

"Last used" is the later of when you last visited the tab's page and, if the profile archives tabs automatically, when the tab was last on screen. Private windows don't show it.

Turn previews off in **Settings → General → Previews on hover**. The tab's name then shows as a plain tooltip.

**Settings → General → Preview Settings…** turns each kind on or off: **Folder previews**, **Unread mail in Gmail** and **Recent pages in Linear, Notion and Figma**. All three are on by default. With folder previews off, a folder shows its name as a tooltip.

Favorite previews stay in the Favorite's own profile, on your Mac. Unread mail is read by the open Gmail page from Gmail itself, with that profile's sign-in, and recent pages come from that profile's history. Nothing is sent to Arx. Private windows don't show them.

### Tabs that aren't loaded

Under **Pages that aren't loaded** in the same sheet, **Without a saved picture** chooses what a tab's or Favorite's card shows when Arx has no picture of its page yet:

- **Use the site's preview image** is the default. Arx reads only the top of the page, its HTML head, for the picture and summary the site publishes for link previews, and shows that picture with the page's title and description. A site that publishes no picture gets a card with the title, site icon and description. The page itself doesn't run: no scripts load and the site can't set cookies. Arx sends the cookies that tab's profile has for the page's own site, so a page you're signed in to shows its own picture. No other site gets them, not even the one the picture is stored on. What the site returns is kept in memory for ten minutes, and goes sooner when you clear that profile's history, cookies or cache.
- **Load the page** loads the page out of sight, silenced, takes a picture and closes it. The page runs as if you'd opened it, with that profile's sign-in, but it isn't added to your history. If it doesn't load, the card uses the site's preview image.
- **Show no picture** asks the site nothing. The card shows the title, site and when you last used the tab.

**Shift + hover loads the page instantly**, below the choice, is off by default. With it on, the card of a page that isn't loaded shows :keys{combo="⇧"} **Load the page**, and a preview you open with :keys{combo="⇧"}, or press it on, loads the page out of sight the same way as **Load the page**, straight away, for a fresh picture, even when the tab already has one and whatever the choice above says. The card shows the saved picture, or keeps the picture's space, until the new one is ready. Private windows never load pages for previews.

Arx is testing this choice. Turning its feature flag off brings back the earlier two switches, **Picture and description from the site**, which asks without your cookies, and **Full previews**. Quit and reopen Arx afterwards:

```bash
defaults write dev.capturecontext.arx ARX_SITE_PREVIEW_IMAGES -string off
```

To go back to the choice:

```bash
defaults delete dev.capturecontext.arx ARX_SITE_PREVIEW_IMAGES
```

Pictures of pages are saved on your Mac with the tab's profile, so a tab keeps its picture after its page unloads or Arx restarts. Clearing a profile's history deletes them (clearing a time range deletes those taken in it), and a deleted profile takes them with it. Private windows never save a picture and show only live ones.

### Live Calendar

Arx is testing Live Calendar. It's on in the beta. Resting on a Google Calendar Favorite shows today as a scrolling day, with a line at the current time, a **+** for a new event and a menu with when it was last read, **Refresh** and **Disable Live Calendar**. Arx reads the day from Google Calendar itself, in that Favorite's profile with its sign-in, and keeps it on your Mac for a few minutes; nothing is sent to Arx. When the calendar isn't open, Arx opens it out of sight, silenced, to read it, then closes it. If the page can't be read, the card says **Couldn't read the calendar** instead of guessing.

To turn it off everywhere, turn off **Live Calendar previews** in **Settings → Advanced → Feature Flags** and relaunch Arx.

## Move around with the keyboard

Click a row or press :keys{combo="Tab"} to reach the sidebar, then:

- :keys{combo="↑"} and :keys{combo="↓"} move between rows, top to bottom, past the insides of closed folders, apart from the tab a closed folder shows. The row you're on has an accent ring.
- :keys{combo="→"} opens a closed folder, and on an open one goes to its first row. :keys{combo="←"} closes an open folder, and on a row inside a folder goes back up to the folder.
- Moving doesn't switch tabs. :keys{combo="Space"} opens the tab you're on, or opens and closes a folder.

Split View rows are skipped; :keys{combo="Tab"} still reaches each of their pages.

## Rename anything

Press :keys{combo="Return"} on a row, or double-click a tab, to rename it. Your title sticks through pinning, unpinning, duplicating and moves to another profile.

## Clicks and drags

A tab or Favorite switches as soon as you press it, so you see its page before you let go. Press a tab and drag it onto the page, and the tab you were on before and the dragged one become a [split](/tabs/split-view), with the dragged tab on the side you dropped it. Any other drag works as it always has: reorder the tab, drop it in a folder or Favorites, or drag it out of the window. A click on a Split View tab shows it without waiting to see whether you double-click; double-click it to rename it. :keys{combo="⌘ Click"} or :keys{combo="⇧ Click"} marks a tab instead of switching.

## Drag, select, move

- Drag rows to reorder them, or into Favorites, Pinned and folders. A tab moves anywhere: between Pinned and the unpinned tabs, into and out of folders, and into a [split](/tabs/split-view), where it moves next to the tab it joins: dropped on an open tab it's unpinned, dropped on a pinned tab it's pinned. A Favorite joins a split from the grid and stays there. A split is one row in Pinned, a folder or the unpinned tabs. A line with a small ring at its start shows where a row will land, and a folder fills with the accent color when the drop will go inside it.
- :keys{combo="⌘ Click"} or :keys{combo="⇧ Space"} marks several rows. They move together, in the order you picked them.
- **Move Items…** (:keys{combo="⇧ ⌘ M"}) moves the marked rows to another space or folder without dragging.
- Dropping into a space of another profile asks first, because the pages reload in that profile and their sign-ins don't carry over. :keys{combo="Esc"} cancels; see [Moving tabs between spaces](/tabs/spaces#moving-tabs-between-spaces).
- If a move can't happen, such as when the tabs changed while you were dragging, a small toast at the top right of the window says so and nothing moves.

## A tab's menu

Right-click a tab for its menu. Every tab's menu, whether it's an open tab, a pinned tab or a Favorite, has the same four parts, in this order, and leaves out what doesn't apply:

1. **The link**: **Copy** (the original, clean, Markdown and proxy links) and **Peek**.
2. **The tab itself**: **Change Icon…**, **Rename…**, **Update Saved URL** for a pinned tab or Favorite, **Mute Tab** or **Unmute Tab** and **Volume…**, **Picture in Picture**, **Unread Badges** and **Always Keep This Site Loaded**.
3. **Where it sits**: **Add a pane**, **Duplicate Tab**, **Add Tab to New Group**, **Move Items…**, **Open in New Window** and **Move to Window**. **Duplicate Tab** puts the copy right below the tab it copies; the copy of a pinned tab or a Favorite becomes the first open tab.
4. **Closing and unpinning**, last: **Close** (or **Reset** to the saved page), **Close Other Tabs** and **Close Tabs Below**, then **Unpin Tab** for a pinned tab. A Favorite ends with **Close and Reset to Saved Page**, **Unpin Tab** and **Remove Favorite**.

- **Close Other Tabs** closes every unpinned tab of the space but this one. **Close Tabs Below** closes the unpinned tabs after it. Pinned tabs and Favorites stay.
- **Move to Window** lists your other windows of the same profile. The tab moves to that window's space and shows there. **Open in New Window** shows it in a new window of the same space, and this window shows the tab next to it.
- **Picture in Picture** holds **Present Media in Picture in Picture** while the tab plays, **Present Tab in Picture in Picture**, and the tab's own [automatic Picture in Picture](/pages/media#one-tabs-own-choice) choice.

Right-click a tab you've marked with others, and the menu acts on all of them, in the same order: **Copy 3 Links**; **Duplicate 3 Tabs**, **Group 3 Tabs**, **Move to…** and **Move 3 Tabs to Window**; then **Pin 3 Tabs** or **Unpin 3 Tabs**, **Archive 3 Tabs**, **Close 3 Tabs** and **Close Other Tabs**. Closing puts unpinned tabs in the archive as one group and resets pinned tabs to their saved page.

Each of these can be undone in one step with :keys{combo="⌘ Z"}, closed tabs included. :keys{combo="⌘ Z"} in either window brings tabs you moved to another window back to the window they came from.

## Undo

Outside anything you're typing in, :keys{combo="⌘ Z"} takes back the last thing you did to your tabs in this window, newest first:

- tabs you closed, while the [Tab Archive](/tabs/archive) still holds them;
- moves, pins and unpins, Favorites, renames, folders, groups and Split Views;
- tabs you moved to another space or window, back where they were, with the space shown again;
- a space you deleted, with its tabs and folders, for a minute;
- a change an assistant made through MCP, which says which assistant made it.

Tabs you already reopened with :keys{combo="⇧ ⌘ T"} aren't reopened twice: :keys{combo="⌘ Z"} goes on to the change before them. What you did since in another window, or what pages loaded, stays as it is.

A text field or editor on a page, the address bar or the chat's box with words in it keep :keys{combo="⌘ Z"} for your typing. **Edit → Undo Organization Change** always takes back the window's last change, wherever you're typing.

One :keys{combo="⌘ Z"} per window is new in the beta and on by default. To turn it off, turn off **One Undo per window** in **Settings → Advanced → Feature Flags** and relaunch Arx, or quit Arx and run:

```bash
defaults write dev.capturecontext.arx ARX_WINDOW_UNDO -string off
```

Off, :keys{combo="⌘ Z"} goes to the page or field you're in, as before, and **Edit → Undo Organization Change** takes back only this window's own last change. A change in another window or by an assistant, a closed tab or a deleted space clears what it could take back, and only the window you moved tabs from brings them back.

## A tab's own icon

Right-click any tab, pinned tab or Favorite and choose **Change Icon…** to give it a symbol or an emoji from the same picker spaces use. It shows instead of the site's icon on the tab's row or the Favorite's tile.

- The icon belongs to the tab, not to the website: other tabs of the same site keep theirs.
- It stays when you quit and reopen Arx. A pinned tab's or Favorite's icon syncs with it through iCloud; an open tab's stays on this Mac, as open tabs do.
- Closing or archiving the tab drops its icon, and reopening it from Recently Closed brings back the site's icon. :keys{combo="⌘ Z"} right after closing brings the tab back with its icon.
- The trash button to the right of the picker's search field puts back the site's icon.

## Unread badges

A tab or Favorite shows a count, or a dot, when its page has something unread: a count in its title, such as "(3) Home / X" or "Inbox (3)", or a badge the site sets for itself. On a row the badge sits where the close button appears when you hover; on a Favorite it's in the corner. Tabs in the background, and ones Arx unloaded, keep showing what they last had. When the site reports nothing unread, the badge goes.

Whether badges show is up to you, at four levels. The most specific one you set wins: a tab over its site, the site over the profile, the profile over Arx as a whole.

- **Arx**: **Settings → General → Tabs → Show unread badges**, on unless you turn it off. Below it are the sites that have a choice of their own, each with **Use Default**.
- **A profile**: **Unread badges** in the profile's settings, under Tabs: **Default**, **Show** or **Hide**.
- **A site**: **Unread badges** in the site menu: **Default**, **Show** or **Hide**. It isn't in private windows.
- **A tab**: **Unread Badges** in the tab's or Favorite's menu: **Default**, **Show** or **Hide**. Each **Default** says what it falls back to.

**Hide** removes the mark altogether. The choice covers the counts and dots on tabs and Favorites; the speaker on a tab playing sound and the blue dot on a Favorite shown in WebKit always show. A profile's choice syncs with the profile through iCloud; the site and tab choices stay on this Mac, and a tab's goes when you close it. Sites you hid before keep hiding their badges.

## A tab's sound

A tab that's playing shows a speaker at the end of its row. Click the speaker to open the tab's volume: a speaker button that mutes or unmutes the tab, and a slider from 0 to 100%. Option-click the speaker to mute or unmute straight away.

- **Mute Tab** and **Unmute Tab** in the tab's menu work on any tab, even one with nothing playing yet, such as a call that hasn't started.
- **Volume…** in the tab's menu opens the same slider, so you can turn a tab down before it plays.
- The volume applies to everything the tab plays, calls included, so you can turn a Google Meet call down even though Meet has no volume of its own. It doesn't change the site's own volume, and it lasts until you quit Arx; it isn't saved per site.
- A muted tab keeps a crossed-out speaker, and a tab you've turned down keeps a quieter one, so you can find them again.

Private windows can't mute or turn down their tabs.

## What's playing

When a tab of the space plays music or video, a small player sits at the bottom of the sidebar with the cover art (or the site's icon), the title and play or pause. When the site has a next track, a skip button sits beside play. It shows one item: the most recently opened one that's playing, or the most recent when nothing plays.

Hover the player and it opens into a stack of every tab in the space with music or video, paused ones included, so you can resume one or move between them. The stack is in the order the media was opened: muting, pausing or playing never moves an item. With more than three items it scrolls. Hover an item to see the artist (or the site), the previous-track button and how far along it is; click the bar to jump, where the site allows it. Click an item to go to its tab.

An item goes away when its tab closes or its media leaves the page. A muted page keeps its place until you unmute it.

The cover art, the artist and the track buttons come from the site, so they show only where it offers them, as music and video sites usually do.

## Width, hiding and the Library

- Drag the sidebar's edge to resize it, and double-click the edge to reset it. Each window keeps its own width.
- The space's profile shows beside the sidebar button at the top, as a quiet smiley and name. Click it to move the space to another profile. In a narrow sidebar a long name is shortened, and the badge hides when the row has no room for it; the window buttons never move.
- :keys{combo="⌘ S"} hides the sidebar. Hover the window's edge on its side to show it. It stays while the pointer is over it, and hides again when you move away.
- In full screen, the space the window buttons take at the top of the sidebar closes. Move the pointer to the top edge of the screen to show them, with the menu bar, over the page.
- The **Library** button at the bottom opens Media, Downloads, [History](/tabs/history), Spaces and the Archive. The sidebar's own contents fade and shrink back slightly as the Library slides in. Right-click it for quick commands such as New Space and Reopen Last Closed. Rest the pointer on the button for a second to see a hint with its name and shortcut (yours, if you've changed it); while a hint is up, it moves straight to the space icons next to it.
- If you've downloaded files in this profile, resting on the Library button fans your five most recent ones out above it instead, newest nearest the button: a thumbnail or the file's icon, its name and how long ago it arrived. Hover a row to see the full name; click it to open the file. The rows fold away once the pointer leaves both the button and them. Files that are still downloading, blocked or moved don't show; private windows show their private transfers.
- With the sidebar hidden, the Library works from the sidebar you showed by hovering: the Library and its page, the Spaces columns included, all keep it on screen. It hides when the pointer leaves that whole area.
