Little Arx is a small, temporary window for links you want to read and close. It doesn't bring your main window forward and doesn't add anything to your spaces.

## Open it

| How | Setting |
| --- | --- |
| :keys{combo="⌥ ⌘ A"}, the File menu or the command bar | |
| A global shortcut from any app | **Enable global Little Arx shortcut**, off by default. It works over other apps' own shortcuts. If another app already uses the same keys, Settings says so, and Arx takes them as soon as that app quits; a shortcut set in System Settings → Keyboard → Keyboard Shortcuts, or by an app that watches every key press, still wins. |
| :keys{combo="⌥ ⌘ Click"} a link | **Open modified-click links in Little Arx**: ⌥⌘ Click (default), ⇧⌥⌘ Click, or Off |
| :keys{combo="⌥ ⌘ Return"} on a link you moved to with :keys{combo="Tab"} | Same setting and keys as the click |
| :keys{combo="⇧ Click"} a link | When **Shift-click a link to open** is set to Little Arx |
| Force Click a link | When **Force Click a link to open** is set to Little Arx |
| Click a link to another site in a Favorite or a pinned tab, or the page there opens a new tab | When **Open links to other sites from Favorites** or **from pinned tabs** is set to Little Arx |
| **Open in Little Arx** in a link's context menu on the page | Not in private windows |
| A link from another app | Through [Air Traffic Control](/profiles/air-traffic-control). Links no rule matches open in Little Arx by default. |
| A file you open with Arx: Finder's **Open With**, `open -a Arx file.html`, or a file dropped on Arx in the Dock | Where links no rule matches open, Little Arx by default, in the space in front. The address field shows the file's path. |
| **Open in Little Arx** in a [Peek](/tabs/peek) | |
| Drag a tab from the sidebar out of every Arx window | Opens where you let go. The tab leaves the sidebar; see [Split View](/tabs/split-view#drag-a-tab-out-of-the-window). |

The settings are in **Settings → Links**. Little Arx isn't available while a private window is in front.

## Desktops

Little Arx opens on the desktop where you clicked the link, on the display with the pointer, even when the main window is on another desktop. It doesn't switch desktops. Every link gets its own Little Arx: one already open on another desktop stays there, and closing a window never brings back one from another desktop. When [Air Traffic Control](/profiles/air-traffic-control) asks for a profile at the pointer, the picker and the Little Arx it opens appear on that same desktop.

Once it's open, a Little Arx window stays on its desktop, like any other window: switching to another desktop, or to the main window in full screen, leaves it where it was. To move it, drag it to another desktop, or use Mission Control or Stage Manager. The bar before it expands opens on whichever desktop you're on. As [Picture in Picture](#picture-in-picture) it follows you to every desktop, full-screen ones included, and back in Little Arx it stays on the desktop it's on then.

## The bar and the window

Little Arx starts as a command bar. Type an address or search, and it expands into a compact window with Back, the address, the site button, a copy-link button and a profile picker. The bar closes when it loses focus, or with :keys{combo="Esc"}.

### The profile

The bar opens in the profile of the space you're looking at: the front Arx window's space, or the main window's. With more than one profile, the bar's bottom-left corner shows that profile, across from **Actions ⌘K**. Click it, or press :keys{combo="⌘ I"}, to list your profiles; type to filter, move with :keys{combo="↑"} and :keys{combo="↓"}, and press :keys{combo="↩"} to choose one, or :keys{combo="Esc"} to close the list. The bar then lists that profile's history, open tabs and favorites, with its site icons and search engine, and nothing from the profile before; what you typed stays. The page opens in that profile, in the space the main window shows if it's that profile's, otherwise in the profile's first space. The choice is for this bar only: the next one starts from the space you're looking at again.

With a single profile there's no picker. If you give :keys{combo="⌘ I"} to a command in **Settings → Shortcuts**, the command keeps the key and the picker shows none.

The site button beside the address opens the same site menu as a tab in a main window: Share, Capture Area, Capture Page, Picture in Picture (which floats the Little Arx window), Reading Mode and Open in WebKit along its top; your extensions; the site's permissions, content blocking, zoom, devices and hidden elements; and Clear Cache, Clear Cookies, Manage Extensions and All Site Settings in its **…** menu. Items that act on a main window aren't there: adding an extension, Translate, and the Hide Element and Dark page buttons. [Hide Element](/pages/content-blocking#hide-an-element-on-a-site) still works in Little Arx from :keys{combo="⇧ ⌘ H"} and **View → Hide Element…**. The menu can reach past the window's edges, as a menu does.

Drag the window by its top bar to move it, from anywhere on it: the address, a button or the space around them. A drag that starts on the address or a button moves the window and doesn't open the address or press the button; a click without moving works as usual.

**Little Arx bar position** sets where the bar appears: top right (default), top center, top left or the center of the screen. Drag the bar by its background to move it while it's open; it stays on screen, and the next bar opens where the setting says again. A Little Arx showing a page moves and opens as before.

Switching the profile reopens the page's URL in that profile; cookies don't come along.

## Picture in Picture

Press :keys{combo="⌥ ⌘ P"} (or choose **Window → Present Tab in Picture in Picture**) to turn the Little Arx window itself into Picture in Picture. It's the same page, without reloading, in a small window that stays on top, follows you to every desktop and settles in a corner; see [Page in Picture in Picture](/pages/media#page-in-picture-in-picture). Its bar keeps Back and Forward and the address, which starts with the space it opens in, shown as its shortcut (**⌃1**, **⌃2**…). Choose another space there, or press :keys{combo="⌘ 1"} to :keys{combo="⌘ 9"}, to open the page in that space, as **Open in** does; it keeps floating, as Picture in Picture of its new tab. A space of another profile asks first.

The button where the window's close buttons would be (or :keys{combo="⌥ ⌘ P"} again) turns it back into Little Arx, where it was and at its size, with **Open in** available again. You can switch back and forth as often as you like. Closing the window in either form closes the page, as closing Little Arx always does.

## Where links open

Links you open from a Little Arx page stay in Little Arx. They never open in the main window unless you ask.

| What the page does | Where it opens |
| --- | --- |
| An ordinary link, a redirect or a form | The same Little Arx |
| A link that opens a new tab, :keys{combo="⌘ Click"} or a script that opens a new window | A new Little Arx. With :keys{combo="⌘ Click"} it opens behind, and the one you're reading keeps the keyboard. |
| A sign-in popup, such as **Sign in with Google** | A new Little Arx that stays connected to the page that opened it. When you finish signing in and the popup closes itself, you're back in the Little Arx you started from, signed in. |

[Peek](/tabs/peek) works as it does anywhere else, and :keys{combo="⌥ ⌘ Click"} still opens a link in another Little Arx.

To send a link to the main window, right-click it and choose **Open in Main Window**. It opens in a new tab in the Little Arx's space, so it stays in the same profile. A link's menu in Little Arx also has **Open Link in New Window**, **Open Link in Private Window**, **Open in Peek** and **Open in Little Arx**. To move the page you're on, use **Open in ‹space›** below.

## Keep the page

**Open in ‹space›** at the top right, or :keys{combo="⌘ O"}, moves the page into that space in the main window, as its first unpinned tab. It's a split button: the arrow on its right lists your other spaces, with a search. Within the same profile the live page moves as it is. Into another profile Arx asks first, because the page reloads there and its sign-ins don't carry over; :keys{combo="Esc"} cancels.

Little Arx windows aren't saved or restored when Arx starts. Pages you visit in Little Arx go into the profile's history, and when you close the window its page goes into the [Tab Archive](/tabs/archive) of the space it was in, so :keys{combo="⇧ ⌘ T"} can bring it back as a tab.

## Archive idle windows

**Settings → Links → Little Arx → Archive Little Arx windows** closes Little Arx windows you haven't used for a while: **Never**, the default, or after 15 minutes, 1 hour, 12 hours or 1 day. A window counts as unused from when you click away from it, wherever it is, until you click back into it. Arx looks every few minutes. An archived window's page goes to the [Tab Archive](/tabs/archive) and Recently Closed, as when you close it yourself.

A window stays open while it has something going on: Peek over it, a page that's loading, playing sound or video, in Picture in Picture or full screen, or asking for a permission, a window it opened that's still open (such as a sign-in), or text you've typed into the page. Its time starts over once that ends. A page that asks to stay keeps its window too; it may show its "Leave site?" question while you're elsewhere.

This is new in the beta and on by default; it archives nothing until you pick a time. To hide it, turn its feature flag off, then quit and reopen Arx:

```bash
defaults write dev.capturecontext.arx ARX_LITTLE_ARX_AUTO_ARCHIVE -string 0
```

To turn it back on:

```bash
defaults delete dev.capturecontext.arx ARX_LITTLE_ARX_AUTO_ARCHIVE
```
