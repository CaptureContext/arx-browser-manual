## Three ways in

| Keys | What it's for |
| --- | --- |
| :keys{combo="⌘ T"} | A new tab. Lists open tabs first, then Favorites, then history, and opens your choice in a new tab. |
| :keys{combo="⌘ K"} | Commands first. An address you submit opens in the current tab. |
| :keys{combo="⌘ L"} or a click on the address | Edit the current address, with the whole URL selected. |

:keys{combo="⌘ T"} and :keys{combo="⌘ K"} open the bar centered near the top of the window, at most 573 points wide, and narrower in a small window. :keys{combo="⌘ L"} opens it over the address instead.

## How it looks

The command bar is a glass panel in a raised frame: the window behind it shows through, blurred, and a light bezel runs round its edge. The highlighted result gets a soft raised highlight in its own colors, and every result shows what :keys{combo="Return"} does with it, with an arrow. The icon at the left of the search field is the highlighted result's: a site's or tab's icon, a command's symbol, or the magnifying glass for a search. It follows the highlight as you move it, and shows the magnifying glass when nothing is highlighted. There's no strip along the bottom. **Actions ⌘K** floats in the panel's bottom-right corner, over the results, while there's a result to act on; clicking it, or pressing :keys{combo="⌘ K"}, turns it into the actions' search field with the [actions](#actions) floating above it. The address field (:keys{combo="⌘ L"}) and Little Arx's launcher are drawn the same way.

## What it finds

- **Tabs** of the current profile. **Switch to Tab** says which space a tab is in.
- **History** of the current profile. A page that's open as a tab is listed twice: the tab, then its history entry right below it. :keys{combo="Return"} on the tab switches to it; on the history entry it opens the page again in a new tab and leaves the open one as it is.
- **Spaces**: **Switch to Space**.
- **Folders** of the current space: **Open Folder**.
- **Commands**, each with its keyboard shortcut and its alias, if you gave it one.
- **Settings**: every setting that Settings' own search finds, such as **Haptics** or **Warn before quitting**, with where it lives, such as **Settings › Advanced**. A setting that's a switch shows whether it's **On** or **Off**. :keys{combo="Return"} opens Settings at that row and highlights it. Settings only show up once you type.

Each result shows where it will open.

## Which comes first

History is ranked by how often you visit a page, how often you typed its address, whether you last saw it in the current space, and how recently. A page you type every day comes before one you opened once, even when the other one has a shorter address. Pages you haven't visited in a while sink: after four days, two weeks, a month and three months. Each profile keeps its own history, so ranking only counts the current profile's visits.

A title change or a reload isn't counted as another visit. An address counts as typed when you open it from the command bar: typed in full, the page you were typing toward, or picked from its history results.

History also finds a page by the words you read on it, not only by its title and address. Type at least three letters; each word you type matches the start of a word on the page, accents aside. Those pages come after the ones whose title or address matched, the most visited first, each with the passage that matched under its title. The Library's History page doesn't search them yet. Arx keeps the words of the last 2,000 pages each profile visited, at most the first 10,000 characters of each, on your Mac only, and never for a private window. Clearing history clears them with it, and **Delete from History** removes a page's words too.

When you [bring in](/start/bring-in) history from Chrome, Arc, Firefox, Safari or another browser, its visit counts come too, and Chrome-based browsers' and Firefox's typed addresses, so ranking is right from the first day. Importing the same history again doesn't count anything twice.

When what you type is exactly the name of a command or a setting, such as `reload page` or `haptics`, that result comes first, ahead of history and search suggestions, and :keys{combo="Return"} runs it. Only an exact [alias](#aliases) comes before it. In a private window, the command bar only knows that window's pages.

## Actions

Every result has more than one thing you can do with it. Each shows what :keys{combo="Return"} does on it, such as **Open**, and **Actions ⌘K** floats in the command bar's bottom-right corner. Press :keys{combo="⌘ K"}, or click **Actions**, and it becomes a search field with the result's actions floating above it. The first one is always what :keys{combo="Return"} does.

::preview{scene="command-bar-actions"}
::

| Result | Actions |
| --- | --- |
| An open tab | **Switch to Tab**, **Open in New Tab**, **Open in Peek**, **Open in Little Arx**, **Copy URL** |
| A history entry | **Open**, **Switch to Tab** when the page is open, **Open in Peek**, **Open in Little Arx**, **Copy URL**, **Delete from History**, **Delete All from** the site |
| A typed address | **Open**, **Open in Peek**, **Open in Little Arx**, **Copy URL** |
| A search: what you typed, a suggestion or a bang's search | **Search**, **Search in Peek**, **Search in Little Arx**, and **Copy URL** for the search you typed |
| A command, setting, space or folder | Its own action |

While what you typed could be an address (it has no spaces and doesn't start with `!`), every result's actions also list **Open as is**, second: it opens exactly what you typed as an address, as :keys{combo="⌘ Return"} does. See [What you type](#what-you-type).

In the address field (:keys{combo="⌘ L"}), :keys{combo="Return"} replaces the current page, so pages also offer **Open in New Tab**, and searches **Search in New Tab**. So do pages and addresses found with :keys{combo="⌘ K"}, where an address opens in the current tab too. **Open in Peek** and **Open in Little Arx** are only offered for web pages, not for a chat.

Each action has its own keys, shown next to it, and they work straight from the command bar without opening the actions:

::shortcut-table
---
rows:
  - [Open as is (what you typed, as an address), ⌘ Return or ⌘-click]
  - [Open in New Tab (text that's no address), ⌘ Return]
  - [Switch to Tab, ⌃ Return]
  - [Open in Peek, ⇧ Return]
  - [Open in Little Arx, ⌥ ⌘ Return]
  - [Copy URL, ⇧ ⌘ C]
  - [Delete from History, ⌃ X]
  - [Delete All from the site, ⌃ ⇧ X]
---
::

They follow the link clicks: :keys{combo="⌘"} for a new tab, :keys{combo="⇧"} for Peek, :keys{combo="⌥ ⌘"} for Little Arx. **Copy URL** uses the same keys as copying the page's address, so while the command bar is open they copy the highlighted result's address instead. If you give a command one of the other keys in **Settings → Shortcuts**, the command keeps it, and the action shows no key.

In the actions, type in the search field at the bottom to filter them, use :keys{combo="↑"} :keys{combo="↓"} and :keys{combo="Return"} to pick one, and press :keys{combo="Esc"} or :keys{combo="⌘ K"} to close them and go back to the results. :keys{combo="Esc"} closes the actions first, then the command bar.

**Delete from History** removes the entry from the profile's history at once, in every window, and the command bar stays open. **Delete All from** removes every entry from that site, with or without `www.`; its other subdomains stay. Neither can be undone.

A private window has no Peek, Little Arx or history to delete, so it offers the rest. In Little Arx, a result can be opened, opened in Peek over the page, or copied.

## Aliases

Give a command an alias in **Settings → Shortcuts**, such as `pin` for **Pin Tab**. Typing the start of an alias lists the command; typing the whole alias puts it first, ahead of search suggestions, so :keys{combo="↩"} runs it. See [Aliases](/start/keyboard-shortcuts#aliases).

## What you type

- A full address opens.
- Text with a dot and no spaces, such as `capturecontext.dev`, opens as `https://…`.
- `localhost`, with a port or a path such as `localhost:3000`, opens as `http://…`, since local servers speak plain HTTP.
- Other kinds of address open too:
  - `about:blank`, a `file:///…` path and `view-source:…` open as pages.
  - `arx://settings/…` and `chrome://settings/…` open Settings. See [Settings addresses](#settings-addresses).
  - Another app's link, such as `slack://…`, `zoommtg://…`, `mailto:…` or `spotify:track:…`, opens in the app macOS assigns to it, the one its row names. When no app on your Mac opens it, it's a search. Arx doesn't ask first: you chose it by typing it. The current page stays as it is, and :keys{combo="⌘ T"} leaves no empty tab behind. [App Links](/profiles/air-traffic-control#app-links) handlers apply to links in pages.
- Text with a colon is still a search when it's no address: `note:milk` and `re:invent` search, because no app opens `note:` or `re:`. `example.com:8080` is a website and its port. `javascript:` never runs from the bar.
- :keys{combo="⌘ Return"} opens exactly what you typed as an address, never a search and never the highlighted result. Text without a scheme gets `https://`, so `intranet/wiki` opens `https://intranet/wiki`. In the address field (:keys{combo="⌘ L"}) it replaces the current page; from :keys{combo="⌘ T"} or :keys{combo="⌘ K"} it opens a new tab. The actions list it as **Open as is**, right after the result's own action, and :keys{combo="⌘"}-clicking a result does the same: so typing `asd` and choosing **Open as is** opens `https://asd`, not a search. Text with spaces isn't an address, so then :keys{combo="⌘ Return"} and a :keys{combo="⌘"}-click are the result's **Open in New Tab**, as before, and a command has neither. **Open in New Tab** stays in each result's actions either way. A command or Copy URL you've bound to :keys{combo="⌘ Return"} in Settings › Shortcuts keeps the key.
- Anything else goes to the profile's [search engine](/command-bar/search-and-bangs).

Whatever else matches, the row that opens what you typed and the row that searches the profile's search engine for exactly that are always among the first five results, so they're never buried under tabs and history. When what you typed is an address, there are both, with the address first. Only an exact [alias](#aliases), a command or setting named exactly what you typed, the page you're [typing toward](#typing-an-address), and the [calculator](#calculator) and [create](#create-something-new) rows come before them. With :keys{combo="⌘ K"}, the matching commands come first, and these rows follow within the first five. The address :keys{combo="⌘ L"} shows keeps its own row at the end until you change it.
- `!gh arx` searches a site directly with a bang. `!gh` alone followed by :keys{combo="Return"}, :keys{combo="Tab"} or :keys{combo="Space"} starts a search within the site, and so does your own search shortcut's keyword followed by :keys{combo="Tab"}. See [Search and bangs](/command-bar/search-and-bangs).
- Any other text followed by :keys{combo="Tab"} starts a chat with the assistant. See [Chat with the assistant](/command-bar/chat).

## Settings addresses

Type `arx://settings` to open Settings. Name a pane after it to open that pane, and a row or page after that to scroll to it and highlight it:
- `arx://settings/advanced` opens Advanced;
- `arx://settings/advanced/protectedMedia` goes to its Protected media switch;
- `arx://settings/links/appLinks` opens App Links.

Chrome's settings addresses work as well, typed as `chrome://settings/…` or as `arx://settings/…`. Each opens Arx's place for the same thing:

| Address | Opens |
| --- | --- |
| `chrome://settings/content/protectedContent` | Settings › Advanced › Protected media |
| `chrome://settings/content/camera`, `…/notifications` and every other `content/…` | the current profile's Site Settings |
| `chrome://settings/cookies` | the current profile's Site Settings |
| `chrome://settings/passwords`, `chrome://password-manager` | the current profile's Passwords |
| `chrome://settings/autofill`, `…/payments`, `…/addresses` | the current profile's Autofill |
| `chrome://settings/clearBrowserData` | Clear Browsing Data, for the current profile |
| `chrome://settings/privacy` | Settings › Privacy |
| `chrome://settings/downloads`, `…/searchEngines` | the current profile's page in Settings › Profiles |
| `chrome://settings/appearance` | Settings › Appearance |
| `chrome://settings/defaultBrowser` | the default browser row |
| `chrome://downloads`, `chrome://history`, `chrome://extensions` | Downloads, History and Extensions |

An address Arx doesn't know opens Settings at its top. The current profile is the profile of the window you typed in. A private window has none, so its profile settings open at Profiles. A `chrome://settings/…` link on a page opens the same way.

Opening these addresses from the bar is behind a feature flag, on by default. To turn it off, turn off **Open addresses with any scheme** in **Settings → Advanced → Feature Flags** and relaunch Arx, or quit Arx and run:

```bash
defaults write dev.capturecontext.arx ARX_SCHEME_ADDRESSES -string off
```

With the flag off, such addresses search as they did before.

## Calculator

Type a sum such as `12*7.5`, `sqrt(2)` or `15% of 80` and the answer is the first result. :keys{combo="Return"} copies it and closes the command bar.

It knows `+ - * / ^`, `×` and `÷`, `mod`, `%` (`50%` is 0.5, and `15% of 80` is 12), `!` for factorials, parentheses, `pi`, `e` and `tau`, and functions such as `sqrt`, `cbrt`, `abs`, `round`, `floor`, `ceil`, `min`, `max`, `pow`, `hypot`, `log` (base 10), `ln`, `log2`, `exp` and the trigonometric ones, in radians. A plain number, such as a year, isn't a sum, so it searches as usual.

## Create something new

Type `new` or `create` and what you want: `new doc`, `new sheet`, `create slides`, `new meeting`, `new issue`. The command bar lists the matching services, and :keys{combo="Return"} opens the page that makes one:

| Type | Lists |
| --- | --- |
| `new doc` | Google Docs, Word |
| `new sheet` | Google Sheets, Excel |
| `new slides` or `new presentation` | Google Slides, PowerPoint |
| `new form` | Google Forms |
| `new meeting` | Google Meet, Google Calendar |
| `new event` | Google Calendar |
| `new page` | Notion, Confluence |
| `new issue` | Linear, Jira |
| `new gist`, `new repo` | GitHub |
| `new figma`, `new board` | Figma, FigJam |

`new` on its own lists them all, after anything else it finds, such as **New Tab**. Any `.new` address, such as `docs.new`, is listed the same way. Nothing is looked up while you type: the list is built into Arx.

## Keys

| Keys | What they do |
| --- | --- |
| :keys{combo="↑"} :keys{combo="↓"} | Move through the results. |
| :keys{combo="Return"} | Follow the highlighted result: a typed address, a suggestion or the page you're typing toward. |
| :keys{combo="⌘ K"} | Show or hide the highlighted result's [actions](#actions). |
| :keys{combo="Tab"} | After a bang such as `!yt`, or your own search shortcut's keyword, start a site search. After any other text, ask the [assistant](/command-bar/chat). Tab never opens a result. |
| :keys{combo="Esc"} | Close the command bar, or close the actions or leave a site search first. |
| :keys{combo="Delete"} in an empty field | The same as :keys{combo="Esc"}: leave a site search first, then close the command bar. In the address field, the address comes back. |

The :keys{combo="Delete"} that clears the last character only deletes it; the next one closes. Holding :keys{combo="Delete"} down to clear what you typed never closes the command bar. Turn this off in **Settings → General → Close with Delete when empty**.

## Typing an address

As you type the start of an address you've visited or have open, such as `cap`, the page you're typing toward, `capturecontext.dev`, moves to the top of the results and is highlighted, as in Safari or Arc, so :keys{combo="Return"} opens it. The field keeps only what you typed: Arx doesn't fill in the rest of the address, so a long address scrolls in the field like any other text.

Only an address that starts with what you typed counts. Until you type a `/`, a site's front page comes before its other pages. Among those, Arx picks the page that [ranks](#which-comes-first) highest, so `news` leads to the news site you type every day rather than a shorter one you visited once; with nothing to tell them apart, the shortest address wins. Text with spaces, a `!bang` or a site search never leads to a page, and neither does the address :keys{combo="⌘ L"} shows until you change it. While you're composing text with an input method, such as Japanese or Chinese, the arrows, :keys{combo="Return"}, :keys{combo="Esc"}, :keys{combo="Delete"} and :keys{combo="Tab"} go to the input method first.

## Search suggestions

As you type, the command bar asks your profile's search engine for search suggestions and lists up to four. Each one searches with your profile's search engine. With :keys{combo="⌘ T"} or :keys{combo="⌘ L"} they come right after the rows that open or search for what you typed; with :keys{combo="⌘ K"} they come after commands and your tabs.

Google, DuckDuckGo, Bing, Brave Search, Ecosia and Startpage each use their own suggestion service. Kagi and Perplexity show local results only. Arx never sends a query to Google as a fallback.

Only the typed text goes to the selected provider, without cookies, credentials or a cache, a short moment after you stop typing. Nothing is sent for an address, whether you typed it or the command bar completed it, such as `capturecontext.dev`, `https://…` or `mailto:…`, nor for the unchanged address :keys{combo="⌘ L"} shows, a site search or a `!bang`. Private windows and Little Arx never ask.

Turn it off per profile in **Settings → Profiles → Search suggestions**.
