Open Settings with :keys{combo="⌘ ,"}. The sidebar has a search at the top, so you can type the name of a setting instead of hunting for it; the categories scroll under it. It finds settings inside the panes too, and pages and sheets such as AI Commands, Proxy Links, Air Traffic Control and Local assistants (MCP), by name or by a related word ("MCP", "alias", "bangs"). Each result shows where the setting lives, such as **Advanced › Local assistants (MCP)**. Click one, or move with :keys{combo="↑"} and :keys{combo="↓"} and press :keys{combo="↩"}: Settings opens the pane, scrolls to the setting and highlights it for a moment, or opens its page or sheet. The [command bar](/command-bar/command-bar#what-it-finds) finds the same settings from any window, with whether a switch is on. You can also type an address there: `arx://settings/advanced` opens a pane, and Chrome's `chrome://settings/…` addresses open Arx's place for the same thing (see [Settings addresses](/command-bar/command-bar#settings-addresses)). Each pane opens with a large icon and title; as you scroll, they move into the left of the bar at the top with the pane's description under the title, and the settings blur out softly under the bar, with no line beneath it. The bar sits level with the sidebar's search, clear of the window's top edge. A pane's own search, such as Shortcuts', is a magnifier button on the right of the bar: click it or press :keys{combo="⌘ F"} and it opens into a wide search field. It stays open while it holds text; clear it or press :keys{combo="Esc"} and it folds back. A list that grows as you use Arx, such as Proxy Links, Search Shortcuts or Air Traffic Control's routes, has a page of its own. In its pane it's one row with a count on the right, such as **3 rules**, and a chevron; click it, or press :keys{combo="↩"} or :keys{combo="→"} when you've moved to it with the keyboard. A page inside a pane, such as Assistant › AI Commands, Links › Proxy Links or a profile's page, has a bar with a **Back** button, the page's title and, under it, the pane it's in; :keys{combo="⌘ ["} goes back too, and so does :keys{combo="←"} unless you're typing in a field. Sheets, such as Preview Settings or a profile's Passwords, have the same bar with a **Close** button (×) and the way you came, such as **Profiles › Work**; :keys{combo="Esc"} closes them too. A sheet's own buttons and search sit on the right of its bar, and its content scrolls under the bar, blurring out softly beneath it.

Each pane is a grouped list: a heading, the settings on a rounded card, and a note under the card when there's something to explain. Switches sit on the right of their row. Text you can type in, such as a rule's name, is a rounded field with its name above the value and a pencil on the right; the field gets a colored ring while you type.

Sheets that ask you for something, such as a Links rule or a site's permission, end the same way: **Cancel** with :keys{combo="esc"}, and the action, such as **Save**, with :keys{combo="↩"} in your accent color, or in red when it removes something. A new profile, renaming or removing a profile or space, and Clear Browsing Data have the bar with **Close** (×) instead, which :keys{combo="esc"} also presses, and their action, such as **Create** or **Clear Data**, alone at the bottom. The action stays grey until what you've typed is complete. Rows that open a page or sheet, such as a profile or **Site Settings**, are highlighted under the pointer and darken as you click.

## Appearance

**Settings → Appearance & Sound → Theme** sets every Arx window to **System** (the default, following your Mac's light or dark appearance), **Light** or **Dark**. Each choice shows a small Arx window in your current window background; the selected one has a colored ring.

Profiles and spaces can choose their own theme, and the narrower one wins:

- **Per profile**, in **Settings → Profiles**: its window background, in the same theme panel as a space.
- **Per space**, from the space's menu → **Appearance**.

A profile or space follows the level above until it chooses its own: **Automatic** at the top of the panel follows it for light or dark, and the colors and texture follow it until you place a dot or turn the texture dial.

### Window background

**Settings → Appearance & Sound → Window background** colors the sidebar and window frame for every profile and space that doesn't choose its own. Page content stays clear. It's the theme panel a space's **Appearance** opens, laid out for the page: the dotted color field beside the preset colors, the wave-shaped intensity slider and the texture dial, described in [Space themes](/tabs/spaces#space-themes), with Match site and the accent color as rows below. The field shows the result as you change it. Light or dark is the **Theme** above, so the panel has no Automatic, light and dark buttons here.

| Setting | Default |
| --- | --- |
| Color | Lavender, at a low intensity |
| Intensity | Low, so the window lets a little of the desktop through |
| Match site | Off |
| Texture | Noise, faint |

**Reset to Default** appears once something differs, and puts it back.

Each profile in **Settings → Profiles** has the same panel, with the Automatic, light and dark buttons. A new profile uses the browser default's colors and texture: click the field to pick its own color, remove the last dot to go back, or right-click the dial and choose **Use the Browser Default's Texture**. **Use Global Appearance** clears all of its own choices. Match site and the accent color show **Inherited** until the profile chooses its own, and **Inherit** goes back.

The see-through window that low intensity gives is being tested and is on in the beta. To turn it off, so every window is solid at any intensity, turn off **Translucent Chrome** in **Settings → Advanced → Feature Flags** and relaunch Arx, or quit Arx and run:

```bash
defaults write dev.capturecontext.arx ARX_TRANSLUCENT_CHROME -string off
```

To turn it back on, turn the flag back on in the same place, or quit Arx and run:

```bash
defaults delete dev.capturecontext.arx ARX_TRANSLUCENT_CHROME
```

### Accent color

**Accent color** sets the color Arx draws its own highlights in: folder icons in the sidebar, focus rings, the selected command, drop targets and unread badges. It's in three places, and the most specific choice wins:

- **Settings → Appearance & Sound → Window background**: **Automatic**, the default, takes the accent from the tint, so the default lavender keeps Arx's usual indigo and an orange tint gets an orange accent. Or pick one of the colors, or any color with the color well.
- **Per profile**, in **Settings → Profiles**, and **per space**, from the space's menu → **Appearance**: **Inherited** until you pick a color. **Inherit** goes back to the level above.

Arx checks each accent against the sidebar behind it and darkens it on a light sidebar, or brightens it on a dark one, just enough to stand out, so a pale yellow still reads. With [Match site](#match-site) on, the accent stays the one you chose while the sidebar follows the page.

The accent color is being tested and is on in the beta. To turn it off, turn off its feature flag in **Settings → Advanced → Feature Flags** and relaunch Arx, or quit Arx and run:

```bash
defaults write dev.capturecontext.arx ARX_ACCENT_COLOR -string off
```

To turn it back on, turn the flag back on in the same place, or quit Arx and run:

```bash
defaults delete dev.capturecontext.arx ARX_ACCENT_COLOR
```

### Match site

With **Match site** on, the sidebar and the frame around the page take the color of the page you're looking at, and ease over when you switch tabs or the page changes its color. Arx uses the color the site declares for browsers (its theme color, in the site's light or dark version to match yours), or else the page's own background.

Like the [accent color](#accent-color), it's in three places, and the most specific choice wins:

- **Settings → Appearance & Sound → Window background**: on or off for every window. It's off until you turn it on.
- **Per profile**, in **Settings → Profiles**, and **per space**, from the space's menu → **Appearance**: **Inherited** until you turn the switch on or off. The switch always shows what the window does. **Inherit** goes back to the level above.

So you can turn it on everywhere and off for one space, or leave it off and turn it on for a single profile. A space that had Match site on before it had these levels keeps it on.

The color is softened and kept within a range of lightness so the sidebar's text and icons stay easy to read: a dark site darkens a light sidebar only a little, and a bright brand color reads as a tint. With **Increase Contrast** on in System Settings → Accessibility → Display, the sidebar keeps its usual lightness and takes even less color. With **Reduce Motion** on, the color changes at once.

Pages without a color of their own (a new tab, Settings, a PDF, a page that didn't load) and a split view showing two different sites use the space's own tint and gradient, which stay set below the switch. Film grain stays as it is.

Match site is being tested and is on in the beta. To turn it off, turn off its feature flag in **Settings → Advanced → Feature Flags** and relaunch Arx, or quit Arx and run:

```bash
defaults write dev.capturecontext.arx ARX_MATCH_SITE_BACKGROUND -string off
```

To turn it back on, turn the flag back on in the same place, or quit Arx and run:

```bash
defaults delete dev.capturecontext.arx ARX_MATCH_SITE_BACKGROUND
```

### Sidebar position

**Settings → Appearance & Sound → Sidebar → Position** puts the sidebar on the **Left** (the default) or the **Right** of every window.

### Sound feedback

**Settings → Appearance & Sound → Sound feedback** is off by default. Turn it on and Arx plays a short, quiet sound when something you did finishes out of sight:

| Sound | When it plays |
| --- | --- |
| Capture | A [page capture](/pages/capture) is copied to the clipboard or saved to a file. |
| Done | A [download](/pages/downloads) finishes, even while Arx is in the background. Several finishing together make one sound. |
| Could not | A download fails, or a capture can't be taken, copied or saved. A download you cancel makes no sound. |

Nothing else makes a sound: switching spaces, splitting, moving a tab to Little Arx, copying a link, opening or closing tabs and loading pages are all things you can see happen.

The sounds play as your Mac's interface sounds. They follow the alert volume and the output device in **System Settings → Sound**, stay silent while your Mac is muted, and stay off while **Play user interface sound effects** is off there. Arx has no volume of its own.

They also stay silent:

- while a tab plays sound, in its page or in Picture in Picture;
- while a tab is in a call, or any app on your Mac is using the microphone;
- while another of them is still playing. The same sound doesn't play twice within two seconds.

This one switch turns them all off.

### Application icon

**Settings → Appearance & Sound → Application icon** picks the icon Arx shows in the Dock while it runs: **Dark**, the default, or **Light**. When Arx isn't running, the Dock and Finder show the icon that matches your Mac's appearance.

## Your identity card

The Arx pane shows a card with the number of profiles, spaces and tabs you have. Flip it over, or cycle through its 11 styles.

Under "arx", every style says **Member since** with a month and year, such as November 2026, written in your language's date format.

- **Before you claim a member number**, it's the month Arx was first installed on this Mac. Arx records that date the first time it opens and never changes it. If you used Arx before it started recording the date, it takes the date Arx's data folder was created on this Mac. Reinstalling the app, moving to a new Mac with Migration Assistant or restoring a Time Machine backup keeps it; deleting Arx's data starts over from that day. It isn't synced through iCloud.
- **Once you claim one**, it's your account's date, the same on every Mac. Claiming sends the day Arx was installed on this Mac, and Arx's server keeps the earliest day any of your Macs sent. Claiming on a second Mac shows your first Mac's date there too; if this Mac had Arx earlier, your account's date moves to it. A Mac that already has its number keeps the date it got when it claimed it.

The share button under the card opens the macOS share sheet with the card as an image. Besides the usual services it offers **Copy**, which puts the image on the clipboard, and **Save…**, which saves it as a PNG.

### Member number

Beside the card, **Member number** says **No member number yet** until you claim one. It gives your card a number of its own, written in the card's top-right corner on every style, such as #42. Until you claim one, that corner is empty.

1. Read the note above the button: Arx asks Apple only to confirm your account, without your name or email, and Arx's server keeps only a hash of it and the day Arx was installed on this Mac. **Privacy Policy** beside the button opens the policy on arxbrowser.net.
2. Click **Get your member number**.
3. Apple's sign-in opens in a small private window. Sign in there; while it's open the section says **Signing in…**. The window keeps nothing: no cookie or sign-in stays in any of your profiles, and it closes by itself once Apple answers.

The number then shows on the card and in the section, as "Member #42", with **Confirmed with Apple** under it. Arx keeps it on this Mac and shows it again after you quit and reopen it, without signing in.

The same account gets the same number on every Mac. The number doesn't sync through iCloud: on another Mac, click **Get your member number** there and sign in with the same account.

If it doesn't work, the section says why, and **Try Again** starts over:

- **Couldn't reach Arx's server.** You're offline, or the server didn't answer.
- **Sign-in was cancelled. Nothing was sent.** You closed the sign-in window.
- **Arx's server didn't accept the sign-in.** Try again later.
- **The sign-in didn't finish.** Apple stopped it, or the window stayed open for ten minutes without an answer.

A build of Arx that can't sign in, such as one you built yourself, says **Sign-in isn't set up in this build of Arx.** See [Privacy and security](/help/privacy#member-number) for what the server keeps.

## Default browser

The Arx pane shows whether Arx opens links from other apps. If another browser does, **Make Default** asks macOS to switch; macOS shows its own confirmation. Onboarding offers the same once.

## Battery Saver

**Settings → General → Battery Saver** chooses when Arx saves power: **When battery is low** (20% or lower, unplugged; the default), **When unplugged**, or **Never**. Battery Saver slows background tabs' timers and animations, lowers how often pages redraw, and freezes tabs in the background, as Chrome's Energy Saver does.

It also turns on whenever your Mac is in Low Power Mode, unless it's set to **Never**. When it turns on, the sidebar says so for a moment.

## Quitting and starting

**Warn before quitting** is on by default, so :keys{combo="⌘ Q"} asks first with a **Quit Arx?** dialog:

- **Quit** or :keys{combo="↩"} quits. Pressing :keys{combo="⌘ Q"} again does the same.
- **Cancel** or :keys{combo="esc"} keeps Arx open.
- **Quit, and don't ask again** quits and turns **Warn before quitting** off. Turn it back on in **Settings → General**.

Quitting stops downloads in progress, paused ones too, and you can resume them from [Downloads](/pages/downloads) the next time you open Arx; the dialog says how many will stop. A download in a private window, or one whose site can't resume it, would be lost, so Arx asks then even with **Warn before quitting** off.

**Settings → Advanced → Restore previous windows at launch** is on by default: Arx reopens the windows, spaces and splits you had open when you quit. Turn it off and Arx starts with a single window. Private windows and Little Arx are never restored.

**Settings → General → Tabs → Open to an empty page at launch** is on by default. When you open Arx after quitting it, every window starts on the empty page: no tab is selected in any space, and every folder is collapsed. Your tabs, folders and spaces are all still there; pick a tab to carry on. Turn it off and Arx comes back to the tab you had open in each space and the folders you left open. A window you open while Arx is running isn't affected, and this happens on each Mac on its own, so it doesn't collapse folders on your other Macs.

## Handoff

**Allow Handoff**, on by default in **Settings → General**, offers the page in the front window to your iPhone, iPad and other Macs signed in to the same Apple Account: it shows in their app switcher or Dock, ready to open in their browser. Private windows are never offered. A page you hand off from Safari on another device opens in Arx's current space, when Arx is your default browser. Handoff also has to be on in **System Settings → General → AirDrop & Handoff** on each device.

## Previews on hover

**Settings → General → Previews on hover** is on by default. Resting the pointer on a tab, Favorite or folder in the sidebar opens its preview. **Preview Settings…** beside it turns folder previews, unread mail in Gmail and recent pages in Linear, Notion and Figma on or off, and chooses what a tab whose page isn't loaded shows when there's no saved picture: the site's preview image (the default), loading the page to take a picture, or no picture. **Shift + hover loads the page instantly**, off by default, loads such a page straight away when you open its preview with :keys{combo="⇧"}. See [Hover previews](/tabs/sidebar#hover-previews).

## Unload inactive tabs

**Settings → General → Tabs → Unload inactive tabs** frees the memory of tabs you haven't looked at for a while. Choose **After 1 hour**, **2 hours**, **3 hours** (the default), **6 hours** or **12 hours**, or **Only when memory runs low**. Whatever you choose, when your Mac runs critically low on memory Arx unloads tabs that have been hidden for a minute; change how long in [Memory](#memory). See [Unloaded tabs](/tabs/sidebar#unloaded-tabs).

## Where new tabs open

**Settings → General → Tabs → New tabs open** places a tab you open with the sidebar's **New Tab** button, :keys{combo="⌘ T"} or the command bar (:keys{combo="⌘ K"}): **At the top** of the space's unpinned tabs (the default, as in Arc), **At the bottom**, or **Below the current tab**. Pinned tabs, Favorites and folders stay above it, and a new tab at the top goes above your tab groups.

A tab a link opens isn't placed by this setting: it goes [next to the tab it came from](/tabs/sidebar#links-that-open-a-new-tab).

Arx is testing this setting. Turning its feature flag off brings back the earlier **New tabs open** setting, with the choice you made before it, which defaults to **At the bottom**. Quit and reopen Arx afterwards:

```bash
defaults write dev.capturecontext.arx ARX_NEW_TABS_AT_TOP -string off
```

To go back:

```bash
defaults delete dev.capturecontext.arx ARX_NEW_TABS_AT_TOP
```

## Switch to tabs links open

**Settings → General → Tabs → When a link opens in a new tab, switch to it** is off by default. Then **Open in New Tab** from a link's menu, :keys{combo="⌘ Click"} and a middle-click open the link behind the page you're reading, right after it in the sidebar, and you stay where you are. Turn it on and those tabs come forward instead, in the same place. :keys{combo="⇧ ⌘ Click"} always does the opposite of the setting. See [Links that open a new tab](/tabs/sidebar#links-that-open-a-new-tab).

## Show where links go

**Settings → Links → Link Status → Show where links go** is on by default. Turn it off to hide the label that shows where a hovered link goes and what a click will do. See [Where a link goes](/tabs/sidebar#where-a-link-goes).

## Unread badges

**Settings → General → Tabs → Show unread badges** turns badges on or off for Arx as a whole. Below it are the sites with a choice of their own, each with **Use Default**. A profile, a site and a tab can each choose differently; see [Unread badges](/tabs/sidebar#unread-badges).

## Close with Delete when empty

**Settings → General → Close with Delete when empty** is on by default. Pressing :keys{combo="Delete"} in an empty command bar or address field closes it, as :keys{combo="Esc"} does. In a site search, the first :keys{combo="Delete"} leaves the search. See [Keys](/command-bar/command-bar#keys).

## Autofill

**Settings → General → Autofill → Ask before filling passwords** is on by default. Picking a saved account in a sign-in form asks for Touch ID or your Mac password before Arx fills it. See [Filling](/profiles/passwords#filling).

**Fill addresses** and **Fill payment cards** below it are on by default. Turn one off and Arx stops offering saved addresses or cards in forms. A card always asks for Touch ID first. See [Addresses and cards](/profiles/addresses-and-cards).

## Haptic feedback

**Settings → Advanced → Haptic feedback** is on by default. On a Force Touch trackpad you feel a tick each time a tab you're dragging in the sidebar would land somewhere new, and a tap when Force Click opens a link in Peek or Little Arx.

## WebKit

**Settings → Advanced → WebKit** has the switch, and **WebKit sites** under it opens the list of sites that open in WebKit pages, where protected video and music play. See [Netflix, Spotify and protected video](/pages/protected-video).

## Memory

**Settings → Advanced → Memory** gathers what Arx keeps in memory:

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unload inactive tabs | After 3 hours | How long a hidden tab keeps its page before Arx unloads it. The same setting as in **General → Tabs**. |
| When memory runs low | After 1 minute | When your Mac runs critically low on memory, hidden tabs unload once they've been hidden this long, from **After 30 seconds** to **After 10 minutes**, sooner than Unload inactive tabs says. A tab you leave and come straight back to stays loaded. |
| Keep pinned tabs loaded | Off | Pinned tabs and Favorites never unload. |
| Sites kept loaded | None | Sites whose tabs never unload, even when memory runs low. The row shows how many and opens their own page, where **Add Site…** opens a form: type a site, such as `music.example.com` or `example.com` (which covers its subdomains), and with more than one profile choose which profile it's for, since each keeps its own sites. Click a listed site to change it or its profile in the same form. **Remove** stops keeping one. **Always Keep This Site Loaded** in a tab's menu adds and removes them too. |
| Pages kept for Back and Forward | 3 pages | How many pages you've left each tab keeps ready, so :keys{combo="⌘ ["} and :keys{combo="⌘ ]"} show them at once, scrolled where you were. **Off**, or 1 to 6 pages. More pages use more memory. Arx reads this when it opens, so a change applies after you quit and reopen it; the row says so until then. |

See [Unloaded tabs](/tabs/sidebar#unloaded-tabs).

## Feature Flags

**Settings → Advanced → Feature Flags** lists features that are still being tested, each with a switch. A row says what the feature does and gives its `ARX_…` name. In the beta, every flag starts on except **Customizable homepage**, which says it's off until it's ready. See [The homepage](/tabs/homepage#customize-it).

- **A change takes effect after a relaunch.** Each row says so. After you change one, **Some changes take effect after Arx relaunches** appears at the top of the page with a **Relaunch** button; click it to quit and reopen Arx.
- **Some carry a warning.** iCloud sync warns that deletions may sync between Macs, and SQLite workspace that it changes where Arx keeps your workspace, so export an archive first.
- **The environment wins.** Each name also works as an environment variable, such as `ARX_READING_MODE=0` when you open Arx from Terminal. A flag set that way shows a lock and **Set by ARX_… in the environment**, and its switch can't change until Arx is opened without it.

Assistants can't turn feature flags on or off.

## Per-profile settings

Each profile in **Settings → Profiles** has its own:

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Search suggestions | On | Sends what you type in the command bar to your profile's search engine for suggestions, where supported. See [Command bar](/command-bar/command-bar#search-suggestions). |
| Automatically archive tabs | After 6 hours | Moves regular tabs you haven't looked at for 6 or 12 hours, 1 day, 7 days or 30 days to the [Tab Archive](/tabs/archive#automatic-archiving), or **Never**. |
| Show the full URL in the toolbar | Off | Shows the whole address in the address bar instead of the site's name. Editing always shows the whole address. |
| Site Settings | Ask; pop-ups blocked; 100% zoom | What each site may use, and page zoom. See [Site settings](/profiles/site-settings). |
| Downloads: Save files to | Your Downloads folder | Where downloads go. See [Downloads](/pages/downloads#where-files-go). |
| Ask where to save each file | Off | Shows a save panel before each download. |
| Clear browsing data | | Removes history, downloads list, cookies, cache, autofill or site settings for a time range. See [Clear browsing data](/profiles/clear-browsing-data). |
