**Show History** opens the Library on its [History](/tabs/history) page: your history by day, newest first, with a search. A private window has no history to show.

:keys{combo="⌃ 1"} to :keys{combo="⌃ 9"} switch to the space in that place of the switcher at the bottom of the sidebar, in the same order as the Library's Spaces. A number with no space there does nothing. If you'd already given one of these keys to another command, your binding keeps it and that space's shortcut starts off. The same goes for :keys{combo="⇧ ⌘ L"}, which opens and closes the Library, and for the keys added later: :keys{combo="⌘ P"}, :keys{combo="⌘ O"}, :keys{combo="⌘ Y"}, :keys{combo="⌥ ⌘ U"}, :keys{combo="⌘ ."}, :keys{combo="⇧ ⌘ ]"}, :keys{combo="⇧ ⌘ ["}, :keys{combo="⌘ 1"} to :keys{combo="⌘ 9"}, :keys{combo="⌥ ⌘ →"} and :keys{combo="⌥ ⌘ ←"}.

:keys{combo="⌥ ⌘ →"} and :keys{combo="⌥ ⌘ ←"} go to the space after and before the one you're in. From the last space, Next Space goes round to the first, and Previous Space from the first to the last.

## Keys a site can use

Many sites use the same keys as Arx: :keys{combo="⌘ K"} for their own command menu or to add a link, :keys{combo="⌘ F"} for their own find, :keys{combo="⌘ B"} for bold, :keys{combo="⌘ S"} to save (in Arx it shows or hides the sidebar). **Conflict resolution** decides, command by command, who gets the keys while a page has focus. Set it with the picker left of each command's alias in **Settings → Shortcuts**; hover over the picker to see what the chosen one does.

- **Arx:** Arx's command runs at once, and the site never gets the keys.
- **Site:** the site gets the keys at once. If it doesn't use them, Arx's command runs, so no command is ever out of reach.
- **Both:** the site gets the keys at once. If it uses them, Arx says so at the top-right corner, such as "Press ⌘K again to open the command bar"; press the keys again while that shows, and Arx's command runs instead, without the site getting them. If the site doesn't use them, Arx's command runs straight away.
- **Delayed:** Arx holds the keys for a moment and says so at the top-right corner. Press them again in that moment, and Arx's command runs; the site gets neither press. Otherwise the site gets the keys when the moment ends, and Arx's command runs then if the site doesn't use them. If you switch tabs or the page changes in that moment, nobody gets them.

The moment is 2 seconds the first time you use a command's keys on a tab, then 1 second.

| Conflict resolution | Commands, unless you change them |
| --- | --- |
| Arx | Back, Forward, Search or Enter URL (:keys{combo="⌘ L"}), Copy Current URL, Copy Original Link as Markdown, and the keys that always stay Arx's (below) |
| Both | Open Command Bar, Toggle Sidebar, Print…, Pin Tab (Bookmark Tab), Duplicate Tab, Open File…, Developer Tools, Inspect Element, View Source, New Split, Close Pane |
| Site | Every other command, Find… and Find Next included |

Changing a command's conflict resolution never changes its keys.

Some keys always stay Arx's, and their picker is greyed out at **Arx**: New Tab, Close Tab, Close Window, New Window, New Incognito Window, Reopen Last Closed Tab, Next Tab, Previous Tab, Quit Arx and New Little Arx window, so a site can never keep you in a tab or stop you leaving. So does :keys{combo="⌃ Tab"}, the recent tabs switcher. A command with no shortcut has no picker until you record one.

Shortcuts without :keys{combo="⌘"}, such as :keys{combo="⌃ 1"} for a space, the split view keys or an F-key, get a picker too once Arx's engine reports them to it; until then they're greyed out at **Arx**.

Commands you've changed, whether their keys, their alias or their conflict resolution, are listed first under **Customized Shortcuts**, and the rest under **Default Shortcuts**. A command moves when you finish changing it, not while you record its keys.

This works wherever a page has focus: main windows, Little Arx and split view. A page in Picture in Picture shows no message, but the second press works the same. The menu bar items always run Arx's command. **Reset All Keyboard Shortcuts** puts every command's conflict resolution back too. The choices are kept on this Mac; they don't sync over iCloud.

:keys{combo="⌘ Z"} and :keys{combo="⇧ ⌘ Z"} work like **Both** in a site that handles them itself, such as a drawing canvas, with one difference: once you've been on the page for more than two seconds, the site keeps them with no message, so undo works there as you'd expect. Just after you arrive on the tab, the first press goes to the site and the message offers Arx's undo, such as after closing a tab by mistake. In a text field, these keys always undo and redo your typing.

The tab keys follow the sidebar's order in the current space: your Favorites, then pinned tabs (inside folders too, open or closed), then the other tabs. Next Tab wraps from the last tab to the first.

While a page floating in [Picture in Picture](/pages/media#page-in-picture-in-picture) is in front, the same keys move it to a space instead: :keys{combo="⌘ 2"} moves it to the second space in the switcher, and :keys{combo="⌘ 9"} to the ninth.

### Recent tabs

:keys{combo="⌃ Tab"} goes back through the window's tabs in the order you last looked at them, as in Arc, so two pages you go back and forth between stay one key apart wherever they are in the sidebar.

- **Tap** :keys{combo="⌃ Tab"} to go straight back to the tab you were on before. Nothing shows.
- **Hold** :keys{combo="⌃"} after pressing Tab, and the recent tabs show over the page, the one you were on before chosen. Each Tab moves one further back, and :keys{combo="⇧"} with Tab moves forward again; the arrow keys move too. Let go of :keys{combo="⌃"} to go to the chosen tab, or click one.
- **Escape** closes the list and leaves you where you were.
- :keys{combo="⌃ ⇧ Tab"} starts from the tab you looked at longest ago.

The list holds the tabs this window showed, from any space, most recent first, then the current space's other tabs in the sidebar's order. A tab of another space takes you to that space. Tabs that aren't loaded are listed dimmed, and stay unloaded until you go to one. Closed tabs leave the list, and so do another window's tabs.

Arx is testing recent tabs. Turning its feature flag off makes :keys{combo="⌃ Tab"} and :keys{combo="⌃ ⇧ Tab"} Next Tab and Previous Tab again, as in Safari and Chrome, beside the keys you choose for them. Quit and reopen Arx afterwards:

```bash
defaults write dev.capturecontext.arx ARX_RECENT_TAB_SWITCHER -string off
```

To go back to recent tabs:

```bash
defaults delete dev.capturecontext.arx ARX_RECENT_TAB_SWITCHER
```

On a pinned tab or a Favorite, :keys{combo="⌘ W"} resets it to its saved page and unloads it. It doesn't delete it.

:keys{combo="⌘ +"} zooms in too. A zoom applies to the whole site; see [Zoom](/pages/zoom).

**Reading Mode** shows the page's article over the page and closes back to it; see [Reading mode](/pages/reading-mode). If you'd already given :keys{combo="⌃ ⌘ R"} to another command, yours stays and Reading Mode starts without a key.

**Print…** prints the page, or the PDF document it shows, in the macOS print panel. **Page Setup…** in the **File** menu sets the paper size and orientation it uses. **Save Page As…** saves the page as a complete web page (with a `_files` folder beside it), its HTML only, a web archive (`.mhtml`) or a PDF; the save shows up in Downloads like any other. Print, Save Page As and View Page Source work on web pages, not Arx's own pages. **Share…** in the **File** menu, the command bar and the page's right-click menu opens the macOS share menu with the page's address and title. Mail and Messages use the title as the subject.

## Mac menus

**Edit → Find** also has **Use Selection for Find**, :keys{combo="⌘ E"}, and **Jump to Selection**, :keys{combo="⌘ J"}. **Spelling and Grammar**, **Substitutions**, **Transformations** and **Speech** use the focused text field's standard macOS actions. Items are unavailable when the focused view doesn't support them. If you give :keys{combo="⌘ E"}, :keys{combo="⌘ J"} or Paste and Match Style's :keys{combo="⌥ ⇧ ⌘ V"} to a command in **Settings → Shortcuts**, the command gets the keys and the menu item shows none.

**Help → Keyboard Shortcuts** opens Settings at Shortcuts. **Arx Manual** opens this manual. **Copy Diagnostics** copies Arx's version and build, the macOS version and the processor architecture. It includes no page addresses or browsing history.

## Right-click menus

::preview{scene="page-context-menus"}
::

What you right-click decides the menu. In [developer mode](/developers/developer-mode), every one ends with **Inspect Element**.

- **The page itself:** **Back**, **Forward**, **Reload**, **Save Page As…**, **Print…**, **Share…** and **View Page Source**.
- **A text field:** spelling suggestions and **Learn Spelling** for a misspelled word, **Look Up** for selected text, **Undo**, **Redo**, **Cut**, **Copy**, **Paste**, **Paste and Match Style** and **Select All**, **Search for** and **Share…** for selected text, and the **Spelling and Grammar**, **Substitutions** and **Speech** menus. macOS adds its own items, such as **Writing Tools** and **AutoFill**. A password field offers only the editing items.
- **Selected text:** **Look Up**, **Copy**, **Search for** (with the profile's search engine, in a new tab), **Share…** and **Speech**.
- **An image:** **Open Image in New Tab**, **Save Image As…**, **Copy Image** and **Copy Image Address**.
- **A video or audio:** **Loop**, **Show Controls**, and for a video **Picture in Picture** (PiP, see [Media](/pages/media)) and **Copy Video Frame**, then **Open in New Tab**, **Save As…** and **Copy Address**.
- **A link:** see [Copy, clean and proxy links](/profiles/links#a-links-menu).

**Save … As…** asks where to save and shows up in Downloads, also in a private window. Search for and the Open in New Tab items work on a window's tabs, not in Peek or Little Arx.

## Commands without a default key

These are in the menus and the command bar, and you can give any of them a key in **Settings → Shortcuts**:

- **Spaces:** New Space. Switch to Space 1 to 9, Next Space and Previous Space have defaults (above) you can change.
- **Tabs:** New Folder, Move Items…, Archive Selected Tab, Archive All Tabs in This Space, Copy Clean Link, Copy Original Link as Markdown, Copy Proxy Link, Copy Proxy Link as Markdown, Share Proxy Link, Route Current URL.
- **Capture:** Capture Visible Area…, Capture Area….
- **Pages:** Save Page As…, Page Setup…, Share…. :keys{combo="⌘ S"} stays Toggle Sidebar and :keys{combo="⇧ ⌘ P"} stays Open Focused Link in Peek, as in Arc, so these two have no key unless you give them one.
- **Windows:** Library, Chats, Downloads, Media, Tab Archive, Reopen Closed Window.
- **Chat:** Pin Chat to This Tab, Open Chat in Tab.
- **Edit:** Undo Organization Change, which :keys{combo="⌘ Z"} does too outside a text field, unless the site uses the key (see [Keys a site can use](#keys-a-site-can-use)). See [Undo](/tabs/sidebar#undo).

## Change a shortcut

**Settings → Shortcuts** lists every command once, with its [conflict resolution](#keys-a-site-can-use), its alias and its keys. Search by name, keys or alias, then click the command's keys, or **Record Shortcut** if it has none, and press the new keys. The button takes your accent color while it listens. Esc cancels.

- A shortcut is one key together with :keys{combo="⌘"} or :keys{combo="⌃"}. The key can be a letter, digit or symbol, an arrow, :keys{combo="↩"}, or F1 to F12. F-keys work alone too.
- A command can have more than one shortcut. **Add Shortcut**, in the keys' right-click menu and under the recording field, records another one beside it; the menu bar shows the first. Recording without Add Shortcut replaces them all.
- If the keys already belong to another command, Arx offers **Replace Assignment**.
- Arx keeps a few for macOS: :keys{combo="⌘ A"}, :keys{combo="⌘ C"}, :keys{combo="⌘ X"}, :keys{combo="⌘ V"}, :keys{combo="⌘ Z"}, :keys{combo="⇧ ⌘ Z"}, :keys{combo="⌘ H"}, :keys{combo="⌥ ⌘ H"} and :keys{combo="⌘ M"}.
- **Remove Shortcut**, **Reset to Default** and **Reset All Keyboard Shortcuts** undo your changes. The first two are also in the keys' right-click menu.

## Aliases

An alias is a word that finds a command in the [command bar](/command-bar/command-bar#aliases). Type it in the **Alias** field of the command's row and press :keys{combo="↩"}. It's saved lowercased; it can't have a space, start with `!`, or be another command's alias. Clear the field to remove it.
