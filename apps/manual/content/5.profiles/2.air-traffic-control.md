When you click a link in another app, Air Traffic Control decides where it opens. Set it up in **Settings → Links → Air Traffic Control**, a page with your routes and a tester.

## Routes

Each route is two lines, checked from the top; the first route that's on and matches wins.

- **URL**: **Domain** matches that host only; **Domain and Subdomains** also matches hosts under it, only at a dot, so `example.com` covers `docs.example.com` but not `myexample.com`. Then the pattern: a host, with an optional port and path, such as `localhost:3000` or `github.com/capturecontext`. Start it with `http://` or `https://` to match only that scheme.
- **Open in**: where the link opens.

| Open in | Where |
| --- | --- |
| Most Recent Space | The space of the window you used last, in any profile |
| Ask for a Destination | Nothing opens until you choose a profile; see below |
| A profile → Most Recent Space | That profile's space you used last, or its first space |
| A profile → one of its spaces | That space |

Except for Ask, a route also names the window: **Main Window** or [Little Arx](/tabs/little-arx). Little Arx opens in the route's space and its profile, so its cookies and sign-ins are that profile's.

The **Domain** and window choices open a short list under the button. Click a choice, or use :keys{combo="↑"} and :keys{combo="↓"} and press :keys{combo="↩"}. Esc closes the list without changing anything.

## Ask for a Destination

A route or **Otherwise** set to **Ask for a Destination** holds the link before it loads, and the Runway, Arx's destination picker, opens at the pointer, over whatever app you're in: a search field, a **Close** button and your profiles, in the same raised glass as the AI chat and Settings. Nothing is requested, no redirect runs and no tab is made in any profile until you choose; then the link opens once, in [Little Arx](/tabs/little-arx), in that profile's most recent space. Use it for sign-in links, such as Google's, which can stop working if they start in one profile and move to another.

- The profile you used last is at the top and already selected, so :keys{combo="↩"} opens the link there.
- :keys{combo="↑"} and :keys{combo="↓"} move the selection. Type to filter by profile or space name. Each row shows the space the link will open in.
- :keys{combo="⌘ 1"} to :keys{combo="⌘ 9"} open the link in the first nine profiles listed, without moving the selection first. Each row shows its key.
- Hovering a row lights it; clicking it opens the link there.
- Esc, **Close**, or clicking anywhere else closes the Runway and drops the link. Nothing opens.
- Six profiles show at once; with more, the list scrolls.

**Settings → Links → Air Traffic Control → Runway appearance** chooses how it looks:

| Appearance | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Regular | A panel that also names the link at the top, and the keys that open and drop it at the bottom. The default. |
| Compact | Only the search field, **Close** and the profiles, narrower, each on its own glass with no panel behind it. |
- The list doesn't bring Arx's other windows forward.
- When several links arrive at once, the list asks about one at a time, in the order they came. Links that don't ask wait their turn.

Links from other apps all go through Air Traffic Control the same way: when Arx is your default browser, from apps that open a link in Arx, and from links you drop on Arx in the Dock. Files you open with Arx (Finder's **Open With**, `open -a`, a file dropped on Arx in the Dock) have no site for a route to match, so they open where links no route matches go, Little Arx by default.

Hosts ignore case and support international domains. A path is case-sensitive and encoded, and matches as a prefix: `/docs` also matches `/docs2`. Query strings and fragments aren't matched; without a port, 80 for HTTP and 443 for HTTPS.

**New Route** adds an empty route at the end; it's saved once its pattern names a host. Changes save as you make them. If one can't be saved, for example a space that was deleted, the route says why. Each route has a switch to turn it off, and its right-click menu moves it up or down.

Some examples:

| URL | Open in |
| --- | --- |
| Domain `github.com/capturecontext` | Work, Main Window |
| Domain and Subdomains `figma.com` | Design → Most Recent Space, Main Window |
| Domain `accounts.google.com` | Ask for a Destination |
| Domain `meet.google.com` | Most Recent Space, Little Arx |
| Domain `localhost:3000` | Side project, Main Window |

## Otherwise

**Otherwise**, at the end of the routes, sends every other link to a space and a window, or **Ask for a Destination** to choose each time. Out of the box, other links open in Little Arx in the most recent space.

## App Links

Some sign-ins end in an app rather than a page: you connect a service in Claude, sign in in Arx, and the last page hands off to the app with a link such as `claude://…`. **App Links** decides what happens to those links. Set them up in **Settings → Links → App Links**, the page under Air Traffic Control.

**New Handler** adds a card. From the top:

1. The app button, then **Name**, **Scheme** and the remove button. The app button shows a plus until you pick an app, then that app's icon.
2. **Host** and **Path prefix**.
3. The links the handler catches, such as `Catches claude://login/callback…`, or the rule the scheme breaks.
4. **Add profile**, and the **Enabled** switch.

Click the app button to pick an app:

- Type to search. The list shows the apps macOS has for the card's scheme, then the installed apps that open links of their own, one row for each scheme an app opens. That includes apps that tell macOS about their links only once they've run, such as Linear. :keys{combo="↑"} and :keys{combo="↓"} move through the list and :keys{combo="↩"} picks.
- **Choose…** picks any app on disk, such as a custom build or a copy of an app for one workspace.
- Picking an app fills in **Name** and **Scheme**, shows the app's icon, and opens the handler's links in that app. A name you wrote yourself stays.
- **System Default** clears the app: links open in whatever app macOS uses for the scheme.

You can also fill in the fields yourself. **Scheme** is the part before `://`, such as `claude`; it starts with a letter, then letters, digits, `+`, `-` or `.`, and can't be `http`, `https`, `file` or another web scheme. **Host** and **Path prefix** are optional and narrow the handler to one host and a path prefix. A new card is saved once it has a scheme.

The first handler that's on and matches wins. Under the card come its routes, read the way link routing reads: each profile's route first, then **Otherwise** for every other page. **Add profile** adds a profile's route just above Otherwise, starting with the same handling. Each route is one of:

- **Open with System Default** opens the app macOS uses for the link, as other browsers do.
- **Open in ‹app›** opens it in the handler's app. **Other App…** picks a different app for just this row, for example the Work copy of Claude for the Work profile.
- **Run script** runs your script instead, so you decide where the link goes, for example to the one Claude window that started the sign-in.

Arx remembers where a chosen app is and follows it if you move or rename it. If it's gone when a link arrives, the prompt says so and offers **Open with System Default**; Arx never opens another app in its place without asking. If no app opens the link at all, the prompt says that too.

The page's profile picks the handling, so sign in from the profile that matches the app window. With [Ask for a Destination](#ask-for-a-destination) you choose that profile when the sign-in link arrives.

| Handler | In Work | In Personal | Otherwise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Claude, `claude` | Run `fxclaude open-url work "$1"` | Run `fxclaude open-url personal "$1"` | Open in Claude |

### Scripts

An empty script field shows an example, `fxclaude open-url work "$1"`. Press :keys{combo="⇥"} (or :keys{combo="→"}) in the empty field to use it; elsewhere Tab moves on as usual. The example uses `open-url`, a command coming to [fxClaude](https://capturecontext.dev/tools/fxclaude) and [fxCodex](https://capturecontext.dev/tools/fxcodex) that hands the link to the Claude or Codex window for one workspace; the App Links footer links here. Until it's there, any command that takes the link as an argument works.

Under the field, **Test** runs the script with a sample link and **Remove** removes a profile's route. If the test fails, its error appears in full below.

The script runs with `zsh`. The link is `$1`; write `"$1"`, in quotes, so it stays one argument. Arx never puts the link into the script's text, so a page can't add commands to it unless your script runs the link itself, for example with `eval`. The script also gets:

| Variable | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `ARX_LINK` | The link, the same as `$1` |
| `ARX_LINK_SCHEME` | The handler's scheme, such as `claude` |
| `ARX_PROFILE_ID`, `ARX_PROFILE_NAME` | The page's profile |
| `ARX_SOURCE_ORIGIN` | The site that opened the link, such as `https://claude.ai`, never the full page address |

Before a script runs, Arx checks the link: it must be a link with the handler's scheme, at most 8 KB, with no line breaks or other control characters. Otherwise Arx offers to open the app instead.

A script that sends a link to another app may show a macOS Automation prompt, such as "Arx wants to control Claude". Allow it to deliver the link. You can change that permission later in **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Automation**.

A script gets 30 seconds; after that Arx stops it. What it prints goes to a log in `~/Library/Logs/Arx/App Links`, one file per run.

### The prompt

Pages can open these links on their own, through a redirect, so Arx asks first, on the page's window, in place of Chrome's **Open "App"?** dialog. The prompt shows the app's icon and the question, the site that asked and the link, then its buttons at the bottom, the main one last:

- **Open app**: **Open "Claude"?**, with **Open Claude**, named after the app you chose or the one macOS uses.
- **Run script**: **Send This Link to Claude Work?**, with **Send to Claude Work**: the handler and the profile. **Show Script** shows what will run.
- :keys{combo="↩"} presses the main button and :keys{combo="esc"} is **Cancel**. The main button waits a moment before it takes a click or ↩, so input meant for the page can't run anything.
- The menu beside the main button has **Copy Link**, which copies the link and leaves the prompt open.
- **Always allow ‹site› to open this** skips the prompt next time that site opens a link for this handler. It's offered for HTTPS sites and local ones (`localhost`), not plain HTTP sites. The handler lists the sites you allowed, each with **Forget**.

If the page moved on before you answered, nothing runs. If the script fails or runs out of time, the prompt shows its error and the end of its log, with **Open ‹app›** to open the app instead: the handler's app, or the one macOS uses.

Private windows never run scripts and never remember a site: they always ask, then open the app.

A link no handler matches gets the same prompt, never Chrome's own dialog. It asks **Open "Linear"?**, naming the app macOS uses for the link, and **Open Linear** opens it there. **Always allow ‹site› to open this** also saves a handler for the link's scheme, with that site allowed, so it appears in **App Links** for you to change. If no app on your Mac opens the link, nothing happens, as in other browsers.

## Test and fix

- The **tester** shows which route wins for a URL, and where it would open, without opening it.
- **Route Current URL** in the Tabs menu runs the current page through your routes, with a review sheet.
- If a destination fails, for example a space you deleted, the link waits in **Incoming Links** in the main window, with retry, override, copy and cancel. Links that ask also wait there when Arx can't show the profile list, as in automated runs.

Routes only apply to links from other apps and to Route Current URL, not to links you click inside Arx, redirects or Peek. App Links apply to a page's links for other apps, clicked or redirected.
