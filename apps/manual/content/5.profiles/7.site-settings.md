Each profile keeps its own site settings. Allowing a site the camera in your work profile doesn't allow it in your personal one, and private windows keep nothing.

Open **Settings → Profiles**, pick a profile, and click **Site Settings** under its data. To change the site you're on, use the [site menu](/pages/site-menu#site-settings) in the address bar; its **All Site Settings** opens this page.

## Permissions

| Permission | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| Camera | Video from your Mac's cameras |
| Microphone | Sound from your microphones |
| Location | Where your Mac is |
| Notifications | Notifications from the site |
| Pop-ups and redirects | Windows a site opens on its own, and where it sends you without a click |
| Clipboard | Reading what you copied |

**By Default** sets what a site gets when it has no setting of its own. Each permission is **Ask**, which shows a prompt the first time a site wants it, or **Block**. Pop-ups and redirects are **Block** or **Allow**.

**Sites** lists every site with a setting of its own. It's where a site lands when you answer its prompt with Allow or Block, and you can change it there:

- Pick **Allow**, **Block** or **Ask** from a permission's menu. Pop-ups and redirects have no prompt, so they take no Ask.
- The minus button removes one setting, so the default applies again. **Remove** next to a site removes all of them.
- **Add Site…** gives a site a setting before it asks. Type a site such as `meet.google.com`; Arx keeps only its address, such as `https://meet.google.com`.
- The magnifier in the sheet's bar opens **Search sites**, which filters the list.

A setting your organization, an extension or parental controls decide shows **Managed** and can't be changed here.

### Arx's own prompt

Arx is testing a prompt of its own for a site that asks for a permission, in the style of its other prompts. It's on in the beta.

The prompt opens on the window of the page that asked, never another one:

- The site's icon with the permission's symbol, and a plain line such as "github.com wants to show notifications". A frame from another site adds the site it's embedded in.
- **Allow**, or :keys{combo="↩"}, allows the site. When the site may be allowed for this visit only, **Allow Once** does that, and the main button reads **Always Allow**.
- **Block** blocks the site.
- **Cancel**, or :keys{combo="esc"}, answers nothing: the site may ask again, and it isn't blocked.
- A location prompt lets you share an approximate or a precise location when the site can have either.

The buttons wait a moment after the prompt appears, so a click or a key meant for the page can't answer it. A site that asks quietly shows **Permission request** in the corner of the window instead, and the prompt only when you click it. Closing the page or leaving it takes the prompt away without an answer. Allow and Block land under **Sites** as before.

To turn it off, turn off its feature flag in **Settings → Advanced → Feature Flags** and relaunch Arx, or quit Arx and run:

```bash
defaults write dev.capturecontext.arx ARX_PAGE_PROMPTS -string off
```

To turn it back on, turn the flag back on in the same place, or quit Arx and run:

```bash
defaults delete dev.capturecontext.arx ARX_PAGE_PROMPTS
```

macOS has its own say too. The first time a site you allowed uses the camera, microphone, location, Bluetooth or devices on your local network, macOS asks whether Arx may use them. Bluetooth also comes up when a site signs you in with a passkey on your phone. Change those answers in **System Settings → Privacy & Security**.

## Page zoom

**Default zoom** applies to every page in the profile, from 25% to 500%. **Add Site…** under **Page Zoom** gives one site its own zoom, and its minus button removes it. Open pages follow at once, without reloading.

[Zoom In and Zoom Out](/pages/zoom) (:keys{combo="⌘ +"} and :keys{combo="⌘ -"}) on a page set its site's zoom here too, and **Actual Size** (:keys{combo="⌘ 0"}) removes it.

## Assistants

An assistant with a whole-profile grant and the browser admin class can read and change these through [`browser_settings`](/assistants/tools#settings). Allowing a site anything but pop-ups always asks you first, whatever your approval policy. See [When Arx asks](/assistants/grants#when-arx-asks).

## Notifications

A site you allow notifications shows them in Notification Center under Arx, with the site's address and its picture. macOS asks once whether Arx may send notifications; you can change that, and the style of its alerts, in **System Settings → Notifications → Arx**.

Click a notification and Arx comes forward with the site's tab:

- When the site picks a tab itself, as Gmail, Slack and most mail and chat apps do, you get that one, in whichever space it's in.
- Otherwise you get the site's tab in the notification's profile: the one you're looking at if it's that site, else its newest tab in the space you're in, then in the spaces you used last.
- A tab that was unloaded loads again. If you closed the site's tab, it opens again as a new tab, in your current space when it uses that profile. To click without that, as in Chrome, turn off **Open the site from its notification** in **Settings → General**; a notification that outlived its page still opens the site, as Chrome does.

Each profile allows and blocks notifications on its own, under **Notifications** above. Private windows never show notifications: sites are told no, as Chrome does in Incognito.

## Devices

**Devices** lists every USB, HID, serial and Bluetooth device a site in this profile may use, under the site's address. **Forget** takes one back. See [USB, Bluetooth and serial devices](/pages/devices).
